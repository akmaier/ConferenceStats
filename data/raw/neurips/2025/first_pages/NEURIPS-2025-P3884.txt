Abstract
Widely adopted evaluation metrics for sparse-view CT reconstruction—such as
Structural Similarity Index Measure and Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio—prioritize
pixel-wise fidelity but often fail to capture the completeness of critical anatomical
structures, particularly small or thin regions that are easily missed. To address
this limitation, we propose a suite of novel anatomy-aware evaluation metrics
designed to assess structural completeness across anatomical structures, including
large organs, small organs, intestines, and vessels. Building on these metrics,
we introduce CARE, a Completeness-Aware Reconstruction Enhancement framework that incorporates structural penalties during training to encourage anatomical
preservation of significant structures. CARE is model-agnostic and can be seamlessly integrated into analytical, implicit, and generative methods. When applied
to these methods, CARE substantially improves structural completeness in CT
reconstructions, achieving up to +32% improvement for large organs, +22% for
small organs, +40% for intestines, and +36% for vessels.
