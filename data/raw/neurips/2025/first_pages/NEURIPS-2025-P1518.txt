Abstract
While recent advancements in generative modeling have significantly improved
text-image alignment, some residual misalignment between text and image representations still remains. Some approaches address this issue by fine-tuning
models in terms of preference optimization, etc., which require tailored datasets.
Orthogonal to these methods, we revisit the challenge from the perspective of
representation alignment—an approach that has gained popularity with the success of REPresentation Alignment (REPA) [46]. We first argue that conventional
text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models, typically trained on paired image and text
data (i.e., positive pairs) by minimizing score matching or flow matching losses,
