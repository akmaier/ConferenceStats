Abstract
Video generative models can be regarded as world simulators due to their ability to capture dynamic, continuous changes inherent in real-world environments.
These models integrate high-dimensional information across visual, temporal,
spatial, and causal dimensions, enabling predictions of subjects in various status.
A natural and valuable research direction is to explore whether a fully trained
video generative model in high-dimensional space can effectively support lowerdimensional tasks such as controllable image generation. In this work, we propose
a paradigm for video-to-image knowledge compression and task adaptation, termed
Dimension-Reduction Attack (DRA-Ctrl), which utilizes the strengths of video
models, including long-range context modeling and flatten full-attention, to perform various generation tasks. Specially, to address the challenging gap between
