Abstract
We advance both the theory and practice of robust ℓp -quasinorm regression for
p ∈ (0, 1] by using novel variants of iteratively reweighted least-squares (IRLS) to
solve the underlying non-smooth problem. In the convex case, p = 1, we prove
that this IRLS variant converges globally at a linear rate under a mild, deterministic
condition on the feature matrix called the stable range space property. In the nonconvex case, p ∈ (0, 1), we prove that under a similar condition, IRLS converges
locally to the global minimizer at a superlinear rate of order 2 − p; the rate becomes
quadratic as p → 0. We showcase the proposed methods in three applications: real
phase retrieval, regression without correspondences, and robust face restoration.
The results show that (1) IRLS can handle a larger number of outliers than other
methods, (2) it is faster than competing methods at the same level of accuracy,
(3) it restores a sparsely corrupted face image with satisfactory visual quality.
