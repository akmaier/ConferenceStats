Introduction

The multi-armed bandit (MAB) problem is a classical sequential decision-making problem in which
an agent tries to maximize a cumulative stochastic reward [27, 23] under uncertainty. This problem,
which is applicable to various areas such as recommender systems, online advertising and clinical
trials, embodies the well known exploration-exploitation trade-off between learning the environment
and acting optimally based on our current knowledge about the environment.
More formally, in the MAB problem at each time t = 1, . . . , T an agent chooses an arm i from the
set [k] = {1, . . . , k} of k arms, and obtains an iid reward rt drawn from the unknown distribution Ri
over {0, 1} with expectation µi = E [Ri ]. Let a∗ = arg maxa µa be an arm with the largest expected
reward, and denote this reward by µ∗ = µa∗ . Let the (suboptimality) gap of an arm a to be the gap
between its expected reward and that of a∗ , i.e., ∆a = µ∗ − µa . The agent’s goal is tohmaximizei

In this work we address the privacy in such a setting. As a motivating example, consider an
advertisement system in which the server presents to each user an advertisement a ∈ [k]. The
user then decides whether to click on the advertisement or not. This click decision depends on
different private characteristic of the user. The user then reports to the server whether it clicked on the
advertisement (in which case its reward is r = 1) or not (r = 0). From this example, it is clear that r
is private information of the user, and using traditional algorithms for the MAB problem incautiously
might leak user-private data.
