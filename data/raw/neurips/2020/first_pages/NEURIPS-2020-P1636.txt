Abstract
Metropolis-Hastings (MH) is a commonly-used MCMC algorithm, but it can be
intractable on large datasets due to requiring computations over the whole dataset.
In this paper, we study minibatch MH methods, which instead use subsamples to
enable scaling. We observe that most existing minibatch MH methods are inexact
(i.e. they may change the target distribution), and show that this inexactness can
cause arbitrarily large errors in inference. We propose a new exact minibatch
MH method, TunaMH, which exposes a tunable trade-off between its batch size
and its theoretically guaranteed convergence rate. We prove a lower bound on
the batch size that any minibatch MH method must use to retain exactness while
guaranteeing fast convergence—the ﬁrst such bound for minibatch MH—and show
TunaMH is asymptotically optimal in terms of the batch size. Empirically, we
show TunaMH outperforms other exact minibatch MH methods on robust linear
regression, truncated Gaussian mixtures, and logistic regression.
