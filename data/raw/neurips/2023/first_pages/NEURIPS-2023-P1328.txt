Abstract
People are relying on AI agents to assist them with various tasks. The human
must know when to rely on the agent, collaborate with the agent, or ignore its
suggestions. In this work, we propose to learn rules grounded in data regions and
described in natural language that illustrate how the human should collaborate
with the AI. Our novel region discovery algorithm finds local regions in the data
as neighborhoods in an embedding space that corrects the human prior. Each
region is then described using an iterative and contrastive procedure where a large
language model describes the region. We then teach these rules to the human via an
onboarding stage. Through user studies on object detection and question-answering
tasks, we show that our method can lead to more accurate human-AI teams. We
also evaluate our region discovery and description algorithms separately.
