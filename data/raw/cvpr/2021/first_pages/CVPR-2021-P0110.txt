Transfer learning with pre-training on large-scale
datasets has played an increasingly significant role in
computer vision and natural language processing recently.
However, as there exist numerous application scenarios
that have distinctive demands such as certain latency constraints and specialized data distributions, it is prohibitively
expensive to take advantage of large-scale pre-training for
per-task requirements. In this paper, we focus on the area
of object detection and present a transfer learning system
named GAIA, which could automatically and efficiently give
birth to customized solutions according to heterogeneous
downstream needs. GAIA is capable of providing powerful pre-trained weights, selecting models that conform to
downstream demands such as latency constraints and specified data domains, and collecting relevant data for practitioners who have very few datapoints for their tasks. With
GAIA, we achieve promising results on COCO, Objects365,
Open Images, Caltech, CityPersons, and UODB which is
a collection of datasets including KITTI, VOC, WiderFace,
DOTA, Clipart, Comic, and more. Taking COCO as an example, GAIA is able to efficiently produce models covering
a wide range of latency from 16ms to 53ms, and yields AP
from 38.2 to 46.5 without whistles and bells. To benefit every practitioner in the community of object detection, GAIA
is released at https://github.com/GAIA-vision.

techniques throughout the history of deep learning. The
paradigm of pre-training on large-scale source datasets and
finetuning on target datasets or tasks is applied over a wide
range of tasks in both computer vision (CV) and natural language processing (NLP). The motivation behind is to endow
