1. Introduction
Gait recognition aims to utilize walking patterns to identify pedestrians without explicit cooperation, thus drawing
rising attention. Current gait recognition research focuses
on in-the-lab [53, 69] and in-the-wild scenarios [73, 76] for
theoretical analysis and practical application, respectively.
The key to addressing gait recognition is to fully capture the effective visual cues of the gait patterns, i.e., the
regions close to the body boundary [39, 60] for both in-thelab scenarios and in-the-wild scenarios. However, the attention analysis [4, 59, 68] on prevailing methods in Fig. 1
