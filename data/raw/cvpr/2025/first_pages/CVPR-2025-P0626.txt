Meanwhile, there is a large volume of unlabelled data
collected from Sentinel missions. These missions mainly
consist of Sentinel-2 acquiring Multi-spectral (MS) imagery
and Sentinel-1 acquiring Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
data. MS imagery provides rich spatial information and
enables the identification and analysis of the material
composition of objects across multiple spectral bands [8].
SAR data offers all-weather, day-and-night radar, allowing
complete monitoring of surface changes regardless of
weather conditions [11]. Therefore, designing multi-modal
foundation models that integrate both data types through
self-supervised pre-training is crucial [7, 8, 21], as it
significantly reduces the amount of annotated data required
for downstream tasks.
Foundation models have achieved remarkable success in
diverse domains, including computer vision and language
processing [13, 26]. However, their development for remote
sensing remains underexplored. Recently, emerging models
have begun to address this gap [4, 7]. These models can
be classified as either uni-modal, which handles a single
type of data, or multi-modal, which integrates multiple data
types. Both typically employ a self-supervised learning
approach. This approach leverages Masked Autoencoder
strategy (MAE) [4, 20], which involves reconstructing
masked portions of the input. For uni-modal models, the
typical input consists of MS images. These images can be
divided into patches across all bands [4] or 3D tube-shaped
patches [9] spanning several adjacent bands. Meanwhile,
multi-modal models integrate SAR data with MS images.
To capture the latent representations between these two
modalities, contrastive learning is commonly applied
alongside MAE [7]. While these models demonstrate
promising results on various downstream tasks, two key
robustness challenges remain: supporting diverse input
types and effectively handling incomplete data.

Foundation models for remote sensing have garnered
increasing attention for their strong performance across
various observation tasks. However, current models lack
robustness in managing diverse input types and handling
incomplete data in downstream tasks. In this paper,
we propose RobSense, a robust multi-modal foundation
model for Multi-spectral and Synthetic Aperture Radar
data. RobSense is designed with modular components
and pre-trained by a combination of temporal multimodal alignment and masked autoencoder strategies on
a huge-scale dataset.
Therefore, it can effectively
support diverse input types, from static to temporal, unimodal to multi-modal. To further handle the incomplete
data, we incorporate two uni-modal latent reconstructors
that recover rich representations from incomplete inputs,
addressing variability in spectral bands and temporal
sequence irregularities. Extensive experiments demonstrate
that RobSense consistently outperforms state-of-the-art
baselines on complete datasets across four input types for
segmentation, classification, and change detection. On
incomplete datasets, RobSense outperforms the baselines
by considerably larger margins when the missing rate
increases. Project page: https://ikhado.github.
