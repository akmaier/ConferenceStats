Multi-Modal Synergistic Implicit Image Enhancement for Efficient Optical Flow
Estimation

1

Abstract
As a fundamental visual task, optical flow estimation has
widespread applications in computer vision. However, it
faces significant challenges under adverse lighting conditions, where low texture and noise make accurate optical
flow estimation particularly difficult. In this paper, we propose an optical flow method that employs implicit image
enhancement through multi-modal synergistic training. To
supplement the scene information missing in the original
low-quality image, we utilize a high-low frequency feature
enhancement network. The enhancement network is implicitly guided by multi-modal data and the specific subsequent
tasks, enabling the model to learn multi-modal knowledge
that enhances feature information suitable for optical flow
estimation during inference. By using RGBD multi-modal
data, the proposed method avoids the reliance on the images captured from the same view, a common limitation in
traditional image enhancement methods. During training,
the encoded features extracted from the enhanced images
are synergistically supervised by features from the RGBD
fusion as well as by the optical flow task. Experiments conducted on both synthetic and real datasets demonstrate that
the proposed method significantly improves performance on
public datasets.

In practice, varying lighting conditions, particularly lowlight environments, present substantial challenges. Under these adverse conditions, image quality deteriorates
rapidly [34], violating the photometric consistency assumption crucial to traditional optical flow methods [42]. Additionally, images captured in adverse illumination often suffer from increased noise and a loss of texture details [4],
which reduces texture consistency across frames and complicates optical flow estimation.
To address the challenges posed by adverse illumination
and enhance performance, most methods focus on developing advanced feature extraction networks to capture distinctive features for optical flow computation [42, 46]. However, the information available in images captured under
poor lighting conditions is inherently limited, imposing an
upper bound on the quality of features these networks can
extract [43]. Consequently, achieving feature quality sufficient for subsequent steps, such as correlation calculations,
becomes challenging, creating a bottleneck in the optical
flow process. Thus, a crucial step toward enhancing optical
flow performance lies in supplementing the data with highquality information to support more accurate optical flow
estimation.

assumptions such as photometric consistency [1, 2]. These
methods have made significant progress in both computational efficiency and processing accuracy, and they were
once the mainstream methods for early optical flow estimation. With the rapid development of deep learning, optical flow estimation techniques based on deep learning have
