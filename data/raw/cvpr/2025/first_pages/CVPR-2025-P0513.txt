Abstract

1. Introduction
RAW imaging under extremely dark conditions seeks to reveal hidden details within dark areas [6], thereby enhancing image quality and playing a crucial role in image enhancement [9, 22], semantic understanding [18, 27, 39],
cross-modality image analysis [21, 31, 32], and related
applications [24, 33]. Compared to image restoration in
the RGB domain, RAW-based methods deliver superior results by preserving uncompressed sensor data [12, 16]. In
contrast, RGB-based methods inherently suffer from irreversible distortions induced by the Image Signal Processing (ISP) pipeline [2], such as noise amplification and compression artifacts, which introduce additional challenges in
learning and optimization [19, 20].

Recently, enhancing image quality in the original RAW domain has garnered significant attention, with denoising and
reconstruction emerging as fundamental tasks. Although
some works attempt to couple these tasks, they primarily focus on cascade learning while neglecting task associativity
within a broader parameter space, leading to suboptimal
performance. This work introduces a novel approach by
rethinking denoising and reconstruction from a “backbonehead” perspective, leveraging the stronger shared parameter space offered by the backbone, compared to the encoder used in existing works. We derive task-specific heads
with fewer parameters to mitigate learning pressure. By incorporating chromaticity-and-noise perception module into
the backbone and introducing task-specific supervision during training, we enable simultaneous high-quality results
for reconstruction and denoising. Additionally, we design
a dual-head interaction module to capture the latent correspondence between the two tasks, significantly enhancing multi-task accuracy. Extensive experiments validate the
superiority of the proposed method. Code is available at:
https://github.com/csmty/CANS.
