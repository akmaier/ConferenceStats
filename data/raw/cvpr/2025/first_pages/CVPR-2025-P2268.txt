Abstract
Recent advancements in diffusion models revolutionize
image generation but pose risks of misuse, such as replicating artworks or generating deepfakes. Existing image protection methods, though effective, struggle to balance protection efficacy, invisibility, and latency, thus limiting practical use. We introduce perturbation pre-training to reduce
latency and propose a mixture-of-perturbations approach
that dynamically adapts to input images to minimize performance degradation. Our novel training strategy computes
protection loss across multiple VAE feature spaces, while
adaptive targeted protection at inference enhances robustness and invisibility. Experiments show comparable protection performance with improved invisibility and drastically
reduced inference time. The code and demo are available
at https://webtoon.github.io/impasto

1. Introduction
Large-scale diffusion models [8, 22, 24, 29] have achieved
remarkable success in the realm of image synthesis task,
revolutionizing the way we create and manipulate digital
imagery [10, 11, 19, 21, 34]. By adopting recently emerged
personalization techniques, it is now possible to develop
diffusion models that can generate images in one’s unique
styles [2, 5, 14, 25, 28]. However, the very capabilities that
make these models so valuable also pose significant risks.
Diffusion models can allow malicious users to replicate an

individual’s artwork without consent, easily stealing their
creative output [27]. Furthermore, diffusion models are exceedingly adept at mimicking faces and objects [14, 25].
This proficiency is not limited to benign applications but
can be extended to the making of deepfakes or fake news.
The implications for societal harm are profound, as these
technologies could be used to undermine public trust, distort political discourse, and violate personal privacy.
Current efforts to protect images against diffusion models have evolved through adversarial perturbation [6]. By
introducing perturbations to a given image, they render the
protected image resistant to diffusion-based personalization methods, causing the diffusion model to generate distorted outputs. Existing methods primarily rely on two approaches: minimizing the distance between the latent features of the protected and a pre-defined target image within
a VAE encoder (e.g. PhotoGuard [26] and Glaze [27]), or
fooling the noise prediction network (e.g. AdvDM [16] and
Anti-DB [30]). Despite numerous advancements, all current frameworks fundamentally start from an approach that
optimizes perturbations during inference [18] when protection is requested, casting doubt on the practicality of these
methods to be used in many real applications.
