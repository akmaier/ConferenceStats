Shading Meets Motion: Self-supervised Indoor 3D Reconstruction Via
Simultaneous Shape-from-Shading and Structure-from-Motion
Guoyu Lu
Intelligent Vision and Sensing Lab
Binghamton University
guoyulu62@gmail.com

Abstract
Scene reconstruction has a wide range of applications
in computer vision and robotics. To build practical constraints and feature correspondences, rich textures and
distinguished gradient variations are particularly required
in classic and learning-based SfM. When building lowtexture regions with repeated patterns, especially mostlywhite indoor rooms, there is a signiﬁcant drop in performance. In this work, we propose Shading-SfM-Net, a
novel framework for simultaneously learning a shape-fromshading network based on the inverse rendering constraint
and a structure-from-motion framework based on warped
keypoint, room layout, and geometric consistency, to improve structure-from-motion and surface reconstruction for
low-texture indoor scenes. Shading-SfM-Net tightly incorporates the surface shape consistency and 3D geometric
registration loss in order to dig into their mutual information and further overcome the instability on ﬂat regions.
We evaluate the proposed framework on texture-less indoor
scenes (NYUv2 and ScanNet), and show that by simultaneously learning shading, motion and shape, our pipeline is
able to achieve state-of-the-art performance with superior
generalization capability for unseen texture-less datasets.

1. Introduction
For robotics in the indoor environment, the ability to understand its surrounding scenes is of utmost importance for
the facilitation of the tasks, such as scene understanding and
navigation [4] [51] [52] [90] [14] [3] [32]. Over the years,
scene reconstruction has become a key area of research in
3D computer vision and robot vision, leading to considerable improvement. For common outdoor environments,
direct geometry-based SfM approaches are usually reliable
and accurate under favorable conditions, such as sufﬁcient
lighting illumination and rich texture information to establish adequate and accurate correspondences [15]. For an
indoor environment, minimum changes in room layout are
required to establish stable correspondences on textured re-

gions. Recent deep learning-based algorithms on monocular video sequences [89] [93] [77] [41] [81] [2] [10] [92]
[85] [65] [18] [47] [49] have achieved notable improvement
on outdoor driving datasets such as KITTI, Cityscapes, etc,
to facilitate autonomous driving tasks [30] [50] [46]. However, improving prediction accuracy on low-texture regions
is still a challenge. For example, a majority of the currently
available methods, e.g., [89] [77], usually ﬂop on textureless indoor datasets such as on NYUv2 or ScanNet.With
those regions (e.g., walls and ﬂoors) frequently containing
a large proportion of few textures or repeated patterns, it
always fails to apply photometric constraints continuously.
In those regions, the estimated inverse depths are generally
inﬁnite or random. However, the photometric error will remain close to zero. If depth estimation is inaccurate, it will
cause a dramatic performance decrease in the camera motion estimation, resulting in an ineffective training process
of the framework.
Existing 3D reconstruction methods mainly rely on textures to build correspondences. To improve the results of
state-of-the-art self-supervised approaches in low-textured
indoor scenes such as walls, ﬂoors, roofs and curtains, we
propose a novel framework to simultaneously learn shapefrom-shading (SfS) and structure-from-motion (SfM). As
SfM and SfS reconstruct 3D scenes from different perspectives and principles, SfM-Net performs well for textured objects and scenes since prominent structures facilitate pixel
matching [34]. However, it suffers from an absence of ﬁne
surface details and tends to be over-smooth. SfS-Net can
help produce a detailed reconstruction. However, the decomposition of the observation to intrinsic images is ambiguous and thus hard to be directly applied in real applications. To tackle this issue, we utilize the shading information to optimize the scene shape estimated from the SfM
network with surface shape and scene registration consistencies. The surface shape consistency beneﬁts from the
