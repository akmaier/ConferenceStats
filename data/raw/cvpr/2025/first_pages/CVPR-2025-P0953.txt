Recently, researchers have drawn inspiration from the success of prompt learning in natural language processing
[11, 22, 23, 33], leading to the innovative approach of visual
prompt learning (VPL) [5, 6, 19]. This method involves
training a global visual prompt, consisting of pixel-level
modifications often represented as padding or patches, using
a pre-trained visual model and task-specific downstream data.
The integration of this visual prompt with any downstream
test image facilitates model predictions. Recent studies have
validated the efficacy of VPL, demonstrating its competitive
advantage over traditional parameter fine-tuning methods
across various applications.
Although VPL has demonstrated significant potential
[5, 6, 19], its practical application encounters substantial barriers, especially when pre-trained models are only available
through opaque black-box APIs or as proprietary software.
To address this challenge, Oh et al. [27] introduce Blackbox Visual Prompting (BlackVIP), an innovative approach
that facilitates effective transfer learning with pre-trained
vision models without requiring direct access to their parameters. However, black-box VPL necessitates considerable
API queries to PTMs, resulting in significant costs. Furthermore, the high dimensionality of continuous prompts poses
challenges for black-box optimization algorithms, as these
methods require a substantial number of queries to optimize
prompts effectively in high-dimensional spaces. Therefore,
improving performance with a limited number of queries is
a key challenge in high-dimensional black-box VPL.
One of the straightforward candidates for addressing the
abovementioned challenge is to adopt the pre-training-finetuning paradigm typical of large language models (LLMs).
Pre-training can yield high-quality initial weights, which in
turn significantly reduces the number of API queries required
during the fine-tuning phase of black-box VPL. Nevertheless, pre-training does not reduce the dimensionality of the
tunable parameters, which can significantly impact the convergence rate of black-box optimization methods. Therefore,

Visual Prompt Learning (VPL) has emerged as a powerful
strategy for harnessing the capabilities of large-scale pretrained models (PTMs) to tackle specific downstream tasks.
However, the opaque nature of PTMs in many real-world
applications has led to a growing interest in gradient-free
approaches within VPL. A significant challenge with existing
black-box VPL methods lies in the high dimensionality of
visual prompts, which necessitates considerable API queries
for tuning, thereby impacting efficiency. To address this issue, we propose a novel query-efficient framework for blackbox visual prompting, designed to generate input-dependent
visual prompts efficiently for large-scale black-box PTMs.
Our framework is built upon the insight of reparameterizing
prompts using neural networks, improving the typical pretraining-fine-tuning paradigm through the subspace learning
strategy to maximize efficiency and adaptability from both
the perspective of initial weights and parameter dimensionality. This tuning intrinsically optimizes low-dimensional
representations within the well-learned subspace, enabling
the efficient adaptation of the network to downstream tasks.
Our approach significantly reduces the necessity for substantial API queries to PTMs, presenting an efficient method for
leveraging large-scale black-box PTMs in visual prompting
tasks. Most experimental results across various benchmarks
demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, showcasing
substantial reductions in the number of required API queries
to PTMs while maintaining or even enhancing performance
on downstream tasks.
