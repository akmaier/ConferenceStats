Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection is crucial for machine
learning models deployed in open-world environments.
However, existing methods often struggle with model overconfidence or rely heavily on empirical energy value estimation, limiting their scalability and generalizability. This
paper introduces DEBO (Dual Energy-Based Model for
Out-of-distribution Detection), a novel approach that addresses these limitations through an innovative dual classifier architecture and a unified energy-based objective function. DEBO enhances the standard classification framework by integrating a dual-purpose output space within
a single classifier. The primary component classifies indistribution (ID) data conventionally, while the secondary
component captures open-world information and estimates
uncertainty. Our method overcomes the dependence of traditional energy model-based OOD detection methods on
empirical energy estimation while maintaining theoretical
guarantees. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that DEBO
promotes low energy and high confidence for ID data, while
simultaneously inducing higher energy and decreased confidence for OOD samples. Extensive experiments conducted
on benchmark datasets reveal that DEBO achieves stateof-the-art OOD detection performance while maintaining
comparable classification accuracy on ID data.

Despite the promising results, existing methods often employ traditional classifiers in OOD detection systems. These

The energy model approach [6, 8, 27, 36] presents an
alternative paradigm, enhancing OOD detection by focusing on reducing the energy of ID data rather than explicitly
optimizing for low OOD confidence. While energy-based
models, such as Joint Energy-based Models (JEM) [8], have
proven effective for OOD detection, they typically rely on
Langevin Monte Carlo during training, resulting in substantial computational overhead and optimization instability. The Energy-Based Out-of-distribution Detection (EBO)
method [22], a representative of energy models applicable
to OOD detection, addresses these limitations by introducing an innovative energy regularization technique. This approach effectively circumvents the unstable training process
inherent in JEM, offering a more efficient solution for OOD
detection tasks. Recently, PEBAL [28] has also adopted the
energy regularization loss proposed by EBO for anomaly
segmentation, further validating the approach’s versatility.
Nevertheless, EBO requires empirical estimation of distinct energy values derived from a pre-trained model for
both ID and OOD data, attempting to optimize ID data
to fall below one threshold while maintaining OOD data
above another. This approach presents two critical challenges: first, the selection of suboptimal energy values may
adversely impact ID classification performance; second, the
energy values exhibit inherent dependencies on both the
dataset and model architecture, fundamentally limiting the
method’s scalability and generalizability across different
applications.

1. Introduction
In open-world environments, machine learning models
must demonstrate dual competency: accurately classifying in-distribution (ID) data and reliably identifying out-ofdistribution (OOD) samples that fall outside known categories. However, contemporary neural networks often exhibit excessive confidence in their predictions, significantly
compromising the effectiveness of OOD detection mechanisms.
