an Occlusion-Aware Neural Domain Adaptation method for

Firstly, by utilizing both the training real images without

the real images with pseudo object pose labels and the synthetic images rendered from the CAD-like radiance field.
Experimental results on three public datasets demonstrate
that ONDA-Pose significantly outperforms the comparative
state-of-the-art methods in most cases.

1. Introduction

methods for 6D object pose estimation can be divided into
two categories: fully-supervised 6D object pose estimation methods [16, 25, 39] and self-supervised 6D object
pose estimation methods [4, 36, 38]. Compared with fullysupervised methods that use high-quality object pose labels
as supervision signals, self-supervised methods are not dependent on such pose labels, hence they have attracted more
and more attention in recent years.
Some existing works [17, 35] for self-supervised 6D
object pose estimation utilize only synthetic data (which
can be easily obtained from the CAD model via tools like
Blender [25] or some other tools [27, 34]) during the training stage. However, their performance is significantly lower
in comparison to the fully-supervised methods [25, 39] due
to the domain gap between real and synthetic data. To address this problem, several recent self-supervised methods
[2, 4] attempt to translate the synthetic images with object
poses generated by the CAD model into the real domain
and then use them for model training, as shown in Fig. 1.
