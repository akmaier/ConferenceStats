NoiseCtrl: A Sampling-Algorithm-Agnostic Conditional Generation Method for
Diffusion Models
Longquan Dai, He Wang, Jinhui Tang *
Nanjing University of Science and Technology
{dailongquan, wanghe, jinhuitang}@njust.edu.cn

Abstract
In training-free conditional generative tasks, diffusion
models utilize differentiable loss functions to steer the generative reverse process, necessitating modifications to sampling algorithms like DDPM and DDIM. However, such adjustments likely reduce flexibility and reliability. In this paper, we propose NoiseCtrl, a sampling-algorithm-agnostic
technique for controlled image generation. Essentially, diffusion models generate denoised results zt−1 by adding a
predicted mean µt with random noise ϵt . NoiseCtrl specifically adjusts the random noise while leaving the underlying
sampling algorithms unchanged. At each step t, NoiseCtrl
converts the unconditional Gaussian noise into conditional
noise ϵ′t by substituting the isotropic Gaussian distribution
with the von Mises–Fisher distribution. This substitution
introduces a directional focus while preserving the randomness required for conditional image generation. Thanks to
this non-intrusive design, NoiseCtrl is straightforward to integrate and has been extensively validated through experiments, demonstrating its adaptability for different diffusion
algorithms and superior performance across various conditional generation tasks.

1. Introduction
In recent years, conditional diffusion models (CDMs) have
achieved notable success. Their impact is particularly visible across a range of low-level vision tasks [6]. This includes advancements in image generation [36], inpainting
[17], virtual dressing [23] and person synthesis[21, 22]. The
achievements, from foundational works [9, 24, 27, 28] to
cutting-edge models [4, 19, 35], highlight their versatility
and effectiveness. Traditional conditional diffusion models, while effective, face challenges due to their reliance
on training. These models often require additional training data and computational resources, which can be costly
and limit their broader application.

and requires extensive experimentation to determine optimal settings. This interdependence between approximation
and sampling highlights their complex relationship and the
need for a more robust solution.
The sampling algorithm of diffusion models consists of
two primary elements: an unconditional latent µt+1 , which
satisfies ∇µt+1 pt (µt+1 ) = 0 to maximize the distribution
pt (zt ), and a Gaussian noise ϵt+1 . The output at step t is
represented as zt = µt+1 +σt+1 ϵt+1 , where σt+1 be a constant. To achieve conditional generation, existing methods
replace the unconditional distribution pt (zt ) with the conditional distribution pt (zt |y). We diverge from this approach
by proposing a two-stage noise sampling process. First, we
uniformly sample a condition. Then, we generate output
samples based on a distribution specific to that condition. In
this two-step context, we argue that zt = µt+1 + σt+1 ϵt+1
remains unconditional because the sampling of ϵt+1 is not
biased towards any specific condition. With a deterministic
condition established in the first stage, devoid of randomness, the noise in the second stage becomes conditional,
introducing a directional bias yet preserving its stochastic
properties. In this paper, we disclose that NoiseCtrl, combining µt+1 with this conditional noise, will produce a result result is attentive to the given condition.
