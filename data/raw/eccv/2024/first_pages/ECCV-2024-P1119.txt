Experimental details

1.1

Experimental settings

In this research, we conduct experiments on widely employed datasets, including
CIFAR-10 [11], CIFAR-100 [11], and Tiny ImageNet [4]. We also utilize popular deep neural networks, i.e., ResNet18 [7], PreActResNet18 [8], and WideResNet34 [17]. During the training process, we employ the SGD optimizer for model
optimization, with the weight decay to 5e-4 and the momentum to 0.9. We set
the total training epoch to 110. The batch size and initial learning rate are configured at 128 and 0.1, respectively. We reduce the learning rate by a factor of
0.1 at the 100th and 105th epochs. All our experiments are conducted on a single
GeForce RTX 3090 GPU.
1.2

Attack illustration

In the generation of adversarial examples, we persist until the final iteration is
reached. When skipping the attack once it is successful, the capacity of withstanding noise interference greatly weakens for adversarial perturbations.

What causes catastrophic overfitting (CO)?

2.1

Relationship between CO and feature activation differences

In our main text, we have demonstrated the association between CO and feature
activation differences, using the CIFAR-10 dataset and ResNet-18 network. Next,
we indicate that this relationship holds across diverse datasets and model structures. Concretely, our experiments are conducted on ResNet-18 with CIFAR-100,
PreActResNet18 with CIFAR-10, PreActResNet18 with CIFAR-100, WideResNet34 with CIFAR-10, and WideResNet34 with CIFAR-100. We can observe
from Figs. 1 ∼ 5 that CO consistently occurs with salient feature activation
differences.
