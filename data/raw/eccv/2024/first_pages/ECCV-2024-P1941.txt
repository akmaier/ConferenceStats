A

Implementation Details

A.1

Transformation Parameter t

In addressing the challenge of object scaling and positioning within a composite
image framework, we follow [2], use a transformation parameter  t = [r, x, y] 
in \protect \mathbb  {R}^3 , encapsulating three degrees of freedom. The scaling ratio  r , constrained
within the open interval (0,1), governs the proportionate scaling of the foreground object. Post-scaling, the dimensions of the scaled foreground entity, denoted by  h  and  w , align as  h = r H  and  w = r W  . It’s imperative to note that
the scaling process maintains the original aspect ratio, hence rendering  w  and  h 
interdependent.
The positioning of the scaled foreground object over the background is determined by coordinates  (x, y) , which define the top-left point of the foreground
region against the background canvas. The parameters  x  and  y , both residing in
the range (0,1), signify the normalized horizontal and vertical positions respectively, calculated as  x = \frac {x}{W - w}  and  y = \frac {y}{H - h}  .
To facilitate gradient backpropagation within our dual-path strategy, we employ an affine transformation, represented by a matrix  A , parameterized by  \Theta .
This parameter matrix is derived as a function of  t :

  \Theta (t) = \begin {bmatrix} \frac {1}{r} & 0 & \left (1 - 2x\right )\left (\frac {1}{r} - 1\right ) \\ 0 & \frac {1}{r} & \left (1 - 2y\right )\left (\frac {1}{r} - 1\right ) \end {bmatrix} 
Utilizing this affine transformation, the foreground image  I_{fg}  and its corresponding mask  M_{fg}  are transformed to  \hat {I}_{fg} = A(I_{fg}; \Theta )  and  M_c = A(M_{fg}; \Theta ) 
respectively. The composite image  I_c  is then a blend of the transformed foreground and the background image  I_{bg}  , formulated as  I_c = M_c \ast \hat {I}_{fg} + (1 - M_c) \ast I_{bg}  ,
