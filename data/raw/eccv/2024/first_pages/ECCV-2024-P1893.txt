Abstract. This supplementary material provides the following information: Section 1 provides the proof of the rank deficiency of the matrix
B (cf . Eq.(10) in the main paper) and the fundamental matrix F. Section 2 provides details on generating the constraints between the fundamental matrix and its corresponding translation for different variants
of the proposed solvers; in particular, we discuss i) the solver for different and unknown focal lengths with known relative depths, ii) the solver
for different and unknown focal lengths where relative depths are computed using local feature scales, the solver for equal and unknown focal
lengths with known relative depths, and iii) the solver for equal and unknown focal lengths that is based on learned depth with unknown scale
factor. Section 3 provides additional results from synthetic experiments.
Section 4 provides more experimental results with real data.

Rank deficiency of the matrices B and F

To prove, let’s assume that four points mij , i = 1, . . . , 4; j = 1, 2, in both views
are not collinear, i.e., we do not have a degenerate configuration.
  [{\M T}]_\times \sigma _{i}\M m_{i2} - \M F \M m_{i1}=\M 0, \; i = 1,\dots 4\\ \label {eq:09} 

(1)

Using the first 9 equations (i = 1, 2, 3), we have
  [{\M T}]_\times [\sigma _{1}\M m_{12},\sigma _{2}\M m_{22},\sigma _{3}\M m_{32}] = \M F [\M m_{11},\M m_{21},\M m_{31}]. \label {eq:s02} 

(2)

Without loss of generality, we can assume that the points m11 , m21 , m31 are not
collinear. Thus we have
  \M F = [{\M T}]_\times [\sigma _{1}\M m_{12},\sigma _{2}\M m_{22},\sigma _{3}\M m_{32}]\cdot [\M m_{11},\M m_{21},\M m_{31}]^{-1}, \label {eq:s03} 

(3)

which means that the fundamental matrix F is already singular. Substituting (3)
into the last three equations (i = 4) in (1), we have
  [{\M T}]_\times (\sigma _{4}\M m_{42} - [\sigma _{1}\M m_{12},\sigma _{2}\M m_{22},\sigma _{3}\M m_{32}]\cdot [\M m_{11},\M m_{21},\M m_{31}]^{-1}\M m_{41} )=\M 0. \label {eq:s04} 

(4)

Note that [T]× is a skew-symmetric of rank-2, thus only two of the three equations in (4) are linearly independent. In general, only 11 of the 12 equations
from (1) are linearly independent.
