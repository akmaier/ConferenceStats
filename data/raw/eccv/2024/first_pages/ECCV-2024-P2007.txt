Glossary of Terms

Considered our paper proposed an early attempt with multiple computer vision techniques applied to archaeology. Here we add a glossary of terms to a
broader interdisciplinary audience, making it more accessible and clearer to nonspecialists in computer vision or artificial intelligence.

Table 1: Glossary of Terms
Glossary

Explanation

MLLM

Multimodal Large Language Model, a type of
AI model capable of processing multiple types
of information, including text and images.

Combinatorial Optimization (CO)

A mathematical method for solving complex selection problems, used in this context for fragment selection.

Siamese Network

A type of neural network architecture used for
comparing similarities between two inputs.

OT Layer

Optimal Transport Layer, a network layer used
for handling cardinality constraints in the optimization process.

Cardinality-constrained Optimization An optimization problem with constraints on
the number and target of selected elements.
Zero-shot Learning

The ability of a model to perform tasks without
specific training on those tasks.

Contour Similarity

In our paper, a measure used to evaluate the
degree of match between the edges of fragments.

Dunhuang Manuscripts

Ancient documents discovered in the Mogao
Caves of Dunhuang, China, dating from the 4th
to 14th centuries. These documents are broken
into fragment pieces and kept in different museum collections due to historical reasons. Our
target is to restore these small fragments that
human experts could hardly resolve.

Outliers

In our paper, refer to fragments that do not
originate form the same document.
