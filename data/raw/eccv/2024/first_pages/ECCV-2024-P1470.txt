Further Explaination of Predefined Attributes

This section delineates attributes that are potentially contributory to the efficacy of camouflage, drawing upon prior studies in biological perception and the
evolution of camouflage [2,4–10]. These attributes have been systematically categorized into three primary subclasses: Surrounding Factors (SF), Camouflaged
Object-Self Factors (COF), and Imaging Quality Factors (IQF), as explicated
below:
For Surrounding Factors, pertaining to environmental and contextual considerations, we delineated:
– Environmental Pattern Matching: The environment matches the visual
patterns of the organism.
Supplement: Enhanced by the organism’s choice of location where its natural
colors blend seamlessly with the surroundings.
– Clutter Noise Disruption: The environment’s varied patterns obscure the
organism’s outline.
Supplement: Most effective in complex, heterogeneous environments that
naturally have a lot of visual "noise."
– Environmental Motion Dazzle: The environment’s static or dynamic
patterns make it difficult to track the organism’s movement.
Supplement: Can be especially effective in environments with moving elements like rippling water or rustling foliage.
– Environmental Shading: The interplay of light and shadow in the surroundings can conceal or distort the visual appearance of the organism.
Supplement: This includes the use of dappled light in a forest or the shadows
of underwater structures.
– Environmental Textures: The physical texture of the environment, such
as bark, leaves, or coral, helps to disguise the organism.
Supplement: The organism can use these textures to their advantage by
resting or moving in areas where their own texture is complementary.
For Camouflaged Object-Self Reasons, which is inherent to the Animal
or Object, we defined:
– Color Matching: The organism’s coloration is similar to the environment.
Supplement: Most effective in environments where the organism can change
colors according to the surrounding hues, like a chameleon.
– Shape Mimicry: The organism’s physical form resembles a common environmental feature.
Supplement: This can range from resembling a twig or leaf to adopting the
appearance of stones or other terrain features.
