Theoretical Analysis

Here we provide an analysis from a theoretical perspective on how meta-optimization
formulated in main paper’s Eq. (4) obtains generalizable feedback in addition
to traditional training feedback. By using first-order Taylor expansion, we can
formulate the estimation of a function f (m) as:
 \label {eq:taylor} \begin {aligned} f(m) \approx f(n) + (m-n) \cdot (\nabla f(n)) \end {aligned} 
where n is a point close to m and ∇f (n) is the first derivative of f at n. If
we regard θ − α∇θ Ltune (θ) in Eq. (4) as m and choose to set n = θ, we can
reformulate our overall meta-optimization objective by using first-order Taylor
expansion approximation as:

 \label {eq:optimization_taylor} \begin {aligned} & \min _{\theta }\; \left \{ \mathcal {L}_{tune}(\theta ) + \sum _{i=1}^N \mathcal {L}_{test}^i \big (\theta - \alpha \nabla _\theta \mathcal {L}_{tune}(\theta ) \big ) \right \} \\ = & \min _{\theta }\; \left \{\mathcal {L}_{tune}(\theta ) + \sum _{i=1}^N \mathcal {L}_{test}^i (\theta ) - \alpha \sum _{i=1}^N \big (\nabla _\theta \mathcal {L}_{tune}(\theta ) \big ) \cdot \big (\nabla _\theta \mathcal {L}_{test}^i(\theta ) \big ) \right \} \end {aligned}
