Implementation Details

For all of our experiments, the video length is set to 10 seconds. During the pretraining stage, we randomly select one video frame with a spatial resolution of
224×224 from all available video frames. For audio preprocessing, we first resample the audio waveform to 16 kHz and then compute the audio spectrogram using PyTorch’s [4] kaldi fbank. This process includes 128 triangular mel-frequency
bins and a frameshift of 10 milliseconds. A 10-second audio spectrogram has a
spatial resolution of 1024 × 128. We follow the standard ViT pipeline, which first
patchifies the data and then computes the self-attention mechanism for both
audio and visual tokens. For the MAE loss, we adopt the same loss function as
used in CAV-MAE [2], applying the stopping gradient before the multimodal
layers. During fine-tuning, similar to CAV-MAE [2], we aggregate all predicted
probabilities across all video frames (i.e., 10 video frames) to make more accurate predictions on video data. This approach may be less effective than the
video encoders used in AV-MAE [1] and MAViL [3]. We include all the detailed
hyper-parameter settings in Table 4, Table 5, and Table 6.
