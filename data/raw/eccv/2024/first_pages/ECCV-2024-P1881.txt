Derivation of Eq. (8)

We show in Fig. A an example of a gradient distribution. To compute ULG, we
define upper bounds of the quantization error for clip-in and clip-out gradients:
1) The clip-in gradients are defined as large gradients inside the quantization
interval, shown in the blue arrow in Fig. A. We represent the quantization step
size (the black arrow in Fig. A) in a b-bit setting as 2γgmax /(2b − 2). As the
gradient moves away from its quantized value, the quantization error increases.
Subsequently, the quantization error is maximized at the transition point (the
red dash line in Fig. A), where the error quantifies the half of quantization step
size. We set the upper bound of quantization error for the clip-in gradient Uin
to γgmax /(2b − 2). 2) For the clip-out gradient, the quantized value is assigned
to each side of the quantization interval (i.e., ±γgmax ). The quantization error
is maximized when the full-precision gradient is located at the minimum or
maximum value of gradients, where the error is estimated as (1 − γ)gmax . Using
the upper bounds of the error for clip-in and clip-out gradients, we define U (GL )
as follows:
