Appendix

1.1

Implementation Details of the Experiments

Our implementations are mainly based on the codebase from ThreeStudio [1].
For all experiments, we use publicly available Stable Diffusion model version 2.1.
We use NVIDIA A6000 GPUs with 48G memory.
1.2

Computational Cost Comparison

The number of required backward and forward diffusion U-Net propagations is
illustrated in Fig A. Compared with the SDS loss baseline, our two proposed
methods require one additional forward and backward propagation to calculate
the updated denoising scores. Compared with the VSD loss, our methods demand
less computation.
3D Experiments with NeRF. We use Instant-NGP as the NeRF backbone
and the similar implementation hyper-parameters as the Prolific Dreamer configurations. Experiments on T3Bench: The embedding based method uses CFG
weight 1000 and embedding learning rate 10-5 to update the learnable embeddings. The LoRA based method uses CFG weight 1000, LoRA weight 0.5 and
LoRA learning rate 5x10-7. When running on A6000 GPUs, Prolific Dreamer
takes around 3 hours to generate one scene, while both of our methods take
only less than 2 hours. Image-to-3D Experiments: We use exactly the same
hyper-parameters as Magic123 with CFG 100. The embedding learning rate is
set to 10-3. The LoRA learning rate is set to 5x10-7.
3D Experiments with 3D Gaussian Splatting. We briefly follow the 3D
Gaussian Splatting implementation of GaussianDreamer [2]. We use Shape-E to
initialize the 3DGS. The images are rendered at a size of 512x512.
