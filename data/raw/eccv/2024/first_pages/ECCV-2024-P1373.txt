Preliminaries

1.1

Disadvantages of Single-View Counting

Most existing approaches compete in the single-view counting (SVC) paradigm
[1, 3, 4, 8, 10, 12–15, 17, 19, 21–23, 31], while the inherent limitations of existing
SVC approaches hinder their practical application, thereby impeding their effective deployment in real-world scenarios. Firstly, SVC methods often struggle
when faced with occlusions, which appear inevitable in real-world situations.
Secondly, the SVC paradigm faces challenges in handling large-scale scenes, as a
single camera can only comprehensively capture part of the scene. Furthermore,
SVC approaches often exhibit imprecise performance when dealing with densely
congested crowd distributions, particularly in the presence of perspective distortions. Moreover, the majority of SVC techniques estimate the density map in
the perspective space, limiting their ability to provide ﬁne-grained information,
such as the density in a speciﬁc spatial area on the ground.
