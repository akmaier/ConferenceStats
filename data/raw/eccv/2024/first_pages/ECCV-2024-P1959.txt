Non-linearity and Conformality of Möbius

Non-linearity Given Möbius transformations for variables  z_1  and  z_2  , Möbius transformation of their sum  z_1 + z_2  :
  \Phi (z_1) = \frac {a z_1 + b}{c z_1 + d},~~~ \Phi (z_2) = \frac {a z_2 + b}{c z_2 + d} 

(4)

  \Phi (z_1 + z_2) = \frac {a (z_1 + z_2) + b}{c (z_1 + z_2) + d} = \frac {a z_1 + a z_2 + b}{c z_1 + c z_2 + d} 

(5)

  \Phi (z_1 + z_2) \neq \Phi (z_1) + \Phi (z_2) \label {eq:non-linear} 

(6)

However, Eq. (6) is not equal to the sum of the individual transformations (Φ(z1 ) +
Φ(z2 )); therefore, non-linear.
Conformality Conformal transformations preserve angles between curves. The angle
between two curves at a point is given by the argument of the complex ratio [19] of
their derivatives. Let f (z) and g(z) represent curves in complex space. If transformation
preserves this ratio, it preserves angles.
Möbius transformation \Phi  of f (z) and g(z) is given as:
  \Phi (f(z)) = \frac {af(z) + b}{cf(z) + d},~~~ \Phi (g(z)) = \frac {ag(z) + b}{cg(z) + d} 

(7)

To find the derivatives of the transformed functions, we apply the chain rule:
  \begin {split} \Phi '(f(z))=\frac {d}{dz}(\Phi (f(z))) = \frac {d}{dz}\left (\frac {af(z) + b}{cf(z) + d}\right )\\ \Phi '(g(z))=\frac {d}{dz}(\Phi (g(z))) = \frac {d}{dz}\left (\frac {ag(z) + b}{cg(z) + d}\right ) \end {split} 
(8)

The angle between the original and transformed curves is given by the argument of
the ratio [19]:
  \begin {split} \text {arg}((f(z))/ g(z)))\equiv \newline \text {arg}\left (\frac {d}{dz}(f(z))\right ) - \text {arg}\left (\frac {d}{dz}(g(z))\right ) \end {split} 

(9)

  \begin {split} \text {arg}(\Phi '(f(z))/ \Phi '(g(z)))\equiv \\ \vspace {-2mm} \text {arg}\left (\frac {d}{dz}(\Phi (f(z)))\right ) - \text {arg}\left (\frac {d}{dz}(\Phi (g(z)))\right ) \end {split} 
(10)

Eq. 11 indicates conformality [37].
  \text {arg}((f(z))/ g(z))) = \text {arg}(\Phi '(f(z))/ \Phi '(g(z))) \label {eq:conform}
