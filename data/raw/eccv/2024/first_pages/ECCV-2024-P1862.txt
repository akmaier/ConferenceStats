Abstract. In this supplementary file, we present additional supporting
materials. Firstly, we provide a detailed overview of all the degradation
tasks outlined in the main paper. Subsequently, we present a visualization
of the degradation similarity. Thirdly, we present more experimental results, including the visual comparison with all-in-one multi-degradation
restoration methods and the quantitative results on the Urban100 test
set. Finally, we conduct ablation studies on the feature extractor, degradation similarity, task grouping, and the threshold of performance drop.

Overview of Degradation Tasks

For this paper, we consider the following 11 typical degradation types.
Gaussian Blur. Gaussian blur is a prevalent image filtering technique utilized
for the purpose of reducing high-frequency noise and suppressing fine details in
an image. It involves the application of a mathematical function, referred to as
the Gaussian kernel, which effectively applies a weighted averaging operation to
the pixel values within a predefined neighborhood. In this paper, we employ an
isotropic Gaussian blur kernel to perform blurring operations on pristine images,
thus synthesizing images with a blurred appearance. For specific parameters, we
set the window size of Gaussian blur to (15,15), and the standard deviation σ of
the kernel to uniformly sampled from [2,4].
Motion Blur. Motion blur arises from the relative motion between the camera
and the scene during the exposure period, resulting in a blurred appearance
of objects or regions in the image. This degradation is particularly relevant in
scenarios involving camera or object motion. In this paper, we convolve the
image with a motion blur kernel, which represents the blurring effect caused by
the relative motion. The kernel size is unifromly sampled from [10,20], while the
kernel angle is uniformly sampled from [0,180].
