Detailed Related Works

We complement more related work on single-stream networks. Grid-based methods, e.g. PixelBert [3], extract image features from convolutional networks and
generate visual tokens by sampling from grid features spatially. Inspired by the
success of the Vision Transformer (ViT) [2], patch-based methods directly concatenate embedded image patches and tokenized text, which abandons the heavier visual encoder and achieves commendable results.
For dual-stream networks, recent works have combined this structure with
other domain, such as UniCL [13] and Florence [14] with contrastive learning,
LiT [15] with larger dataset JFT300M [11], SLIP [9] and DeClip [8] with selfsupervised learning, MVP [12] and BeiTv2 [10] with masked image models.
Pre-training tasks. Multimodal learning employs various pre-training tasks
to enhance joint multimodal representations. Commonly used pre-training tasks
include masked language modeling (MLM), image-text matching (ITM), and
image-text contrastive (ITC) learning. Different methods propose additional pretraining tasks based on their motivation and downstream tasks, including masked
image modeling (MIM) [1], word patch alignment (WPA) [4], language modeling
(LM) [5], and frame order modeling (FOM) [7], etc.

Implementation Details

Our experimental setting primarily follows [6]. We utilize the PyTorch framework, conducting all experiments on 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs. The text encoder
and fusion encoder employ the Bert-base model (123.7M parameters), while the
image encoder employs a 12-layer ViT-B/16 model (85.8M parameters). For all
momentum encoders, we employ a momentum parameter of m = 0.995 and set
the feature queue size Q to 65536. We employ the AdamW optimizer with a
weight decay of 0.02. For the learning rate schedule, we set the initial learning
rate to 1e-5, warmed up to 1e-4 after 1,000 iterations, and then followed a cosine
decay to the minimum learning rate of 1e-5. We pre-train for 30 epochs. During
