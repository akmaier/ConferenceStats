Group theory

This section mainly summarizes previous works [2, 3, 14] focused on theoretical underpinnings or implementations of the group convolution. In Section 1.1, we
present the fundamental concepts and terminologies from those previous works.
Then we derive the equivalence of convolutions on group and quotient space,
justifying the filter function design of CSEConv by extending the formulation of
the filter from this derivation in Section 1.2.
1.1

Concepts to derive group convolution

Through this section, we denote G as a group and H as its subgroup.
Section map: Before defining a section map, we first need to define a canonical
projection p : G → G/H. A canonical projection maps a group element g to the
element of quotient space, or the coset referencing g: p(g) = gH. Accordingly,
the definition of the section map is as follows:
  s: G/H \to G\;\;\text {s.t.}\;p \circ s = \text {id}_{G/H}, 

(1)

where idG/H is an identity function over G/H. It indicates that s maps an
input coset to the one of group elements that belong to the coset. Generally,
multiple group elements comprise a single coset element, which obscures a unique
mathematical formulation of the section map, unlike a canonical projection.
Therefore, a section map is determined by the specific implementation choice.
Additionally, we define a new function h : G/H × G → H with a section map
for the derivation in supplementary Section 1.2, proposed from [2, 3]:
  h(x, g) \triangleq s(gx)^{-1}gs(x), 

(2)

which is a difference in H caused by a section map in the group space G when
x ∈ G/H and g ∈ G are specified.
