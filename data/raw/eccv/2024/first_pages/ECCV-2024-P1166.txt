Overview

This supplementary material shows the additional implementation details of our
method, baseline methods, and our collected dataset. Additional results about
comparison, are also presented to further demonstrate the superior performance
of our method. We strongly encourage the reader to see our video supplementary, in which we present our results on the test scene and comparisons with
baselines.

Implemention Details

Our code is built upon the Pytorch implementation of NeRF (https://github.com/
yenchenlin/nerf-pytorch). During the training and testing, the rays are mapped
from camera space to the normalized device coordinate (NDC) space [5]. It is
interesting to note that our camera correction module can be used in various
NeRF-based models, such as Instant-NGP [6], tri-plane [2], and mip-NeRF [1].
An eight-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) with 256 channels takes the encoding of position x as input to generate σ. This is followed by one additional 256channel fully-connected layer to extract a feature. The diffuse color cd is determined by mapping this feature to RGB channels. To obtain the view-dependent
color cs , an extra 128-channel fully connected layer and a mapping layer are
employed, taking both the previously mentioned feature and the encoding of the
view direction d as input. Both cd and cs are transformed to nonlinear space by
the kernel mapping function Φ,
  \Phi ([\mathrm {R,G,B}]^T)\rightarrow [\mathrm {R,G,B,RG,RB,GB,1}]^\top . 

(1)

The embedding vector is reshaped to color correction matrix M of size 3 × 7.
Models have trained over 300,000 iterations with a batch size of 1024.
For fairness, the baseline methods, including NeRF [5], NeRF-W [4], and HaNeRF [3], both transform the ray to the normalized device coordinate (NDC)
