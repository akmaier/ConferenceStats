Architecture of Image Denoising Baseline

We use NAFNet [1], a 4-level encoder-decoder, as our image denoising baseline.
We use the residual blocks introduced in [1]. The number of residual blocks in
the encoder from level-1 to level-4 is [2, 2, 4, 6], with the channel number set
to [64, 128, 256, 512] correspondingly. In the decoder, the number of residual
blocks from level-3 to level-1 is [2, 2, 2].

2

Selection of Noise Models

During fine-tuning, the noise sampled from the noise model is utilized to corrupt
the pseudo clean video. For synthetic Gaussian video denoising, we use additive
white Gaussian noise (AWGN):
  n_i \sim \mathcal {N}(0,\sigma ^2), 

(1)

where ni denotes the noise value at pixel i, and σ is the standard deviation of
AWGN. For a noisy video with noise level σ, we first denoise the video with
our pre-trained blind Gaussian denoiser. Subsequently, noise sampled from the
AWGN at the same noise level σ is added to the denoised video to construct
pseudo noisy-clean video pairs. For real raw video denoising, we use PoissonGaussian noise:
  n_i \sim \alpha \mathcal {P}(x_i) - x_i + \mathcal {N}(0,\delta ^2), 
(2)
where P denotes the Poisson noise, xi is the image intensity at pixel i, and α, δ
are parameters related to ISO. These parameters can be calibrated following
previous works [7, 9]. In particular, the CRVD dataset [8] provides the camera
parameters for various ISO levels, which can be directly utilized to synthesize
the noise to the pseudo clean video.
