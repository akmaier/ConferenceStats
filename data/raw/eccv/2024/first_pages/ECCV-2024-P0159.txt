Fig. 1: With a random pool of 1000 sequences, we observe that the least active joints
can be viewed as static reference points, showing minimal movement in NTU RGB+D
120. In contrast, Assembly101 exhibits subtler distinctions between the most active
and the least active joints. The Pearson correlation coefficient (r) between the distance
values for these two joints yields a high value (0.93) for Assembly101, while r = 0.33
for NTU RGB+D 120. These results suggest strong coupling among hand joints during
motion, emphasizing the dominance of full-skeleton motion in hand poses. Our method
leverages this understanding, balancing long-term motion patterns and short-term articulation changes by factorization.
