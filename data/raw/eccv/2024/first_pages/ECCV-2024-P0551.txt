Scaled Dot-Product Attention

We first have a brief review of the standard scaled dot-product attention [7].
The input consists of query vectors, key vectors with the dimension of Dqk , and
value vectors with the dimension of Dv . To handle multiple query, key, and value
qk
vectors in parallel, the vectors are packed into matrixes Q = [q i ∈ RD : i =
qk
v
1, . . . , N q ], K = [ki ∈ RD : i = 1, . . . , N kv ], V = [v i ∈ RD : i = 1, . . . , N kv ].
The scaled dot-product attention is defined as:
  \mathtt {Attention}(\boldsymbol {Q}, \boldsymbol {K}, \boldsymbol {V})=\mathtt {Softmax}\left (\frac {\boldsymbol {Q} \boldsymbol {K}^\text {T}}{\sqrt {D^\text {qk}}}\right ) \boldsymbol {V} 

(1)

The attention function can not capture the order information of the input,
or more specifically, the pixel location. Therefore, we follow the prior work to
add the positional embedding [7] to the queries and keys.

Padding Mask

Padding masks are used in the original Transformer to mask out the semantically
meaningless part of the input [7]. In our work, we adopt the padding mask to
mask out the background pixels from the input pixel sequence. We convert the
predicted saliency map S ∈ [0, 1]H×W into a padding mask M with a dimension
of W H. Let si and mi respectively denote the ith element of S and M . The
padding mask is constructed as:
  m_i= \begin {cases} 0 & \text { if } s_i \geqslant \alpha \\ -\infty & \text { if } s_i<\alpha \end {cases}
