Boundary Evaluation Protocol

This section addresses the challenges of directly applying traditional boundary
discontinuity metrics [8, 13, 14] to high-resolution depth estimation. Then, we
introduce our approach, which utilizes high-resolution segmentation masks as
proxies for assessing depth boundary quality. Our method computes the precision, recall, and F-1 Score metrics introduced in our paper and a refined depth
boundary error metric detailed here. Together, this enables a comprehensive
boundary evaluation on real-domain datasets.
Depth Boundary Error (DBE) is a standard metric for assessing depth discontinuity accuracy [8, 13, 14]. It involves extracting boundary masks M̃ and M
from the ground-truth (GT) and predicted depth maps, D̃ and D, using methods like structured edges [4, 8], Sobel [7, 13, 14], or Canny operators [2, 13, 14].
DBE calculates the truncated chamfer distance between these masks, employing an Euclidean Distance Transform (EDT) on edge maps while disregarding
distances beyond a threshold θ to focus on the local edge evaluation. The accucomp
racy measure εacc
DBE and the completeness error εDBE quantify the proximity of
predicted boundaries to the ground truth and vice versa, respectively. They are
defined as
  \varepsilon ^{acc}_{DBE} = \sum \mathrm {EDT}(\Tilde {\mathbf {M}}(p))\cdot \mathbf {M}(p), 

(1)

  \varepsilon ^{comp}_{DBE} = \sum \mathrm {EDT}(\mathbf {M}(p))\cdot \Tilde {\mathbf {M}}(p). 

(2)

These metrics were proposed as part of the IBims-1 [8] benchmark, and
recently adopted in monocular depth estimation challenges [13, 14]. We refer to
these papers for more details.
However, directly computing edge information on GT depth maps is impractical due missing values in the GT depth map. These missing values often occur
close to edges, as shown in Fig. 1 (samples from CityScapes [3] obtained by stereo
GT system). Due to the missing values it is not possible to locate where exactly
the edge is. Moreover, this issue also affects depth maps from LiDAR or scene
reconstructions, characterized by sparsity and missing data (e.g., ETH3D [11],
KITTI [6] and high-resolution depth in ScanNet++ [15]). On the other hand,
low-resolution depth maps are naturally not precise enough on boundaries for
high-resolution depth prediction evaluation (e.g., low-resolution depth in ScanNet++ [15] and NYU [12]).
