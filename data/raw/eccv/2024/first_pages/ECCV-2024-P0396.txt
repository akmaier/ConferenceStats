In the main paper, we point out that the fusion between two input images
including Softmax Splatting [10] and time-weighted averaging loses details and
high-frequency information. Here, we give an easy mathematical explanation.
In the theories of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models [4], a clean image
can be turned into a full Gaussian noise by constant noise addition. Since our
fusion process is performed at the noisy timestamp, we may as well assume that
the noisy latent codes are standard Gaussian noise.
Now we have the noisy latent code for each of the two images to be interpolated, zT0 ∼ N (0, 1) and zT1 ∼ N (0, 1), where T is the noisy timestamp. According
to the nature of the Gaussian distribution, if the whole latent obeys the standard
Gaussian distribution, each pixel should also conform to the standard Gaussian
distribution and each pixel is independent of each other. Consider the Softmax
Splatting operation and assume that the pixel points r1 , r2 , . . . rn ∼ N (0, 1) from
zT0 are mapped to the same location by optical flow F 0→δ , where δ ∈ (0, 1) is the
middle time to be interpolated. Then the value of this location is the weighted
average of the pixel points, and the formula is:

where µi > 0 is the weight of ri and µ1 + µ2 + · · · + µn = 1. Due to the additivity of independent Gaussian distributions, the mean at this location remains
unchanged but the variance becomes µ21 + µ22 + · · · + µ2n , which is far less than the
original 1. Thus, the variance of all pixels of zT0→δ obtained by Softmax Splatting
of zT0 is much less than 1. In the same way, we can get zT1→δ obtained by Softmax
Splatting of zT1 .
