Handling flips

As detailed in the main manuscript, flips require special care. Let (Ii , Ij ) be a
source and target images respectively (for simplicity, we drop the DINO ViT
and learned features notion and use this notion). In this particular case, our loss
function reduces to
 \label {eq:loss:full:supmat} \Lcal _\mathrm {IC} =\ellTwoNorm {I_j - (I_i \circ T^{\btheta _i} \circ T^{-\btheta {_j}}) }^2 \, .

(1)

To incorporate flips efficiently, we consider only horizontal flips (since vertical
flips could be reached through a horizontal flip + rotation) and compute the
gradient only between the best matching pair. Particularly, let F ki be the k th
flip configuration applied to the ith image, where k ∈ C such that C holds the
possible configuration (in our case, 2). The objective function is now

 \label {eq:loss:flips:supmat} \Lcal _\mathrm {IC} =\sum _{i=1}^{N}\sum _{k_i=1}^{|C|}\min _{k_j \in C } \ellTwoNorm {I_j\circ F^{k_j} - ((I_i \circ T^{\btheta _i} )\circ F^{k_i})\circ T^{-\btheta {_j}} }^2 \, ,

(2)

where (ki , kj ) are the flips considered for the image pair (Ii , Ij ).

Curriculum learning

To incorporate the Lie-algebraic curriculum learning during training, we gradually add more complex transformation modules, starting from SE(2) and later
“release" more of the transformation parameters, to obtain (invertible) affine
transformations and finally homographies. Figure 1 illustrate the process, where
additional transformation parameters are “released", as illustrated by the warped
