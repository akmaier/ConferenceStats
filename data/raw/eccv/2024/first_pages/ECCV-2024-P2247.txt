Appendix: Implementation Details

A.1

Deep Monocular SLAM System

The SC-SfMLearner is modified to use a ResNext50-32⇥4d [79] encoder, with a
DeepLabV3+ [17] depth decoder, and the pose decoder acting on the concatenation of features from two images extracted by the backbone instead of training
a separate pose prediction network. To deal with distortion eﬀects caused by
diﬀraction of using a camera without a dome port under water, we employ the
universal camera model (UCM) [22] instead of linear intrinsics.
We follow Sauder et al. [62] in extracting video frames at 20 FPS and
608 ⇥ 352px resolution, which keeps the aspect ratio as close as possible to the
full inputs (1920 ⇥ 1080px), while being a multiple of 16, which is required for
the ResNeXt backbone architecture. We train the SC-SfMLearner for 2 million
iterations using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0002 and a batch
normalization momentum of 0.01. We use the universal camera model with a
learnable distortion parameter that converges to -0.4407. For data augmentation, the input image pairs are randomly horizontally flipped, and individually
color-jittered for depth and pose prediction, but the non-jittered images are used
to calculate the loss.
A.2

CausticsNet

CausticsNet uses a ResNext50-32⇥4d [79] backbone, with a DeepLabV3+ [17]
decoder. CausticsNet was trained using Adam optimizer with a learning rate of
0.0002 for 400000 iterations with a batch normalization momentum of 0.01. The
regularization weights were experimentally chosen as T V = 1, `2 = 0.05, [0,1] =
1. The overlapping frame pairs to train CausticsNet were randomly chosen 3 to
6 frames apart, which is equivalent to a random temporal distance between 0.15s
and 0.3s. The frame pairs are color-jittered with the same values for data augmentation and randomly horizontally flipped.
A.3

BackscatterNet

BackscatterNet uses a ResNext50-32⇥4d [79] encoder with a lightweight decoder
of the same structure as the pose decoder of Bian et al. [11], consisting of three
convolutional layers with batch normalization and ReLU activations followed
by mean-pooling along the spatial dimensions. BackscatterNet was trained using Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.0002 for 400000 iterations with
a batch normalization momentum of 0.01. The regularization weights were experimentally chosen as I = 0.1, B = 0.5, [0,1] = 1. The overlapping frame
pairs to train BackscatterNet were chosen randomly between 3 and 25 frames
apart, which is equivalent to a temporal distance of 0.15s to 1.25s. As the SCSfMLearner only leads to reliable camera pose transform estimates for camera
pose changes, the camera pose transform between the two images is computed
