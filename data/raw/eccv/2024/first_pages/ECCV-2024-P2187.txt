Abstract. We provide further details of our proposed method in the
supplementary material, which is divided into the following sections: (1)
Re-balancing loss functions presents a detailed analysis of the impact of different re-balancing losses. (2) Hyperparameter analysis reports the results of our hyperparameter experiments. (3) Comparison
of computational complexity presents a comparison of the computational complexity of our Mg-RMPN with various methods. (4) Qualitative analysis presents a qualitative analysis of scene graph generation
samples through visualization. (5) Limitations summarized the limitations of our method.

1

Re-Balancing Loss Functions

Due to the imbalanced distribution of relation classes in the SGG dataset, the
predictions are dominated by a large number of head-class samples. Therefore,
this paper uses seesaw loss [4] to dynamically adjust the gradients of samples
of different classes. To further analyze the impact of different re-balancing loss
functions, we conducted ablation experiments in Table 1. For cross-entropy and
focal losses, which did not use a re-balancing strategy, excellent results were
achieved in terms of R@K representing head-class performance, but they performed poorly in terms of mR@K, which represents tail-class performance.
