In this supplementary document, we provide additional experiments and
settings for CatchBackdoor. We first present the pseudo-code in Section 1. Next,
we describe the experimental details in Section 2. A running example is provided
in Section 3. More experimental results of the effectiveness of CatchBackdoor are
shown in Section 4, followed by a detailed investigation of neuron behaviors in
Section 5. An intuitive understanding of CatchBackdoor is given in both the input
feature space and the high-dimensional representative feature space in hidden
layers in Section 6. Additionally, The detection results for the online model zoo
are presented in Section 7, and experiments against possible adaptive attacks are
detailed in Section 8. We also use images generated by CatchBackdoor to repair
the model in Section 9. Time complexity is discussed in Section 10. Finally, we
evaluate the effect of random noise triggers on the benign path in Section 11 and
visualize the top-5 neural paths in Section 12.

CatchBackdoor Algorithm

1.1

Benign Path Construction

Constructing a benign path is crucial for ensuring network integrity. This process
involves identifying and linking key neurons that significantly impact the network’s
output, while excluding neurons from the fully connected layer to maintain
path diversity. The pseudocode for constructing a benign path is outlined in
Algorithm 1.
