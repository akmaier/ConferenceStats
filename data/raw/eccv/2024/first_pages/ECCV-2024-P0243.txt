Rendering Audio with Learned Soundfield

As illustrated in Sec 3.2, the sound pressure, i.e. the audio signal, produced by
a learned soundfield of order N is given by
  \begin {split} \mathbf {w}(r,\theta ,\varphi ) & = \sum _{n=0}^{N} \sum _{m=-n}^{n} \left ({c}_{nm} \cdot h_n (kr_{ref})\right ) \cdot \frac {h_n(kr)}{h_n(kr_{ref}) } \cdot Y_{nm} (\theta , \varphi ) \\ & = \sum _{n=0}^{N} \sum _{m=-n}^{n} \Tilde {c}_{nm} \cdot \frac {h_n (kr)}{h_n(kr_{ref}) } \cdot Y_{nm} (\theta , \varphi ), \end {split} \label {eq:primitives} 
(1)

here, k = 2πf /vsound is the corresponding wavenumber; Ynm (θ, φ) represents
the spherical harmonic of order n and degree m, which is
  Y_{nm} (\theta , \varphi ) \equiv \sqrt {\frac {2n+1}{4\pi }\frac {(n-m)!}{(n+m)!}}P_{nm}(\cos \theta )e^{im\varphi }, 

(2)

Pnm (z) is an associated Legendre polynomial, and hn (kr) is nth-order spherical
Hankel functions. All the functions are implemented with PyTorch and, therefore
are fully differentiable. In this paper, we choose spherical harmonics up to second
order and showcase them in Fig. 1.
