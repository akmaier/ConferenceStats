More Experimental Results

1.1

Different Emotion Classifiers in Two-Stage Method

In the main paper, all two-stage methods [1, 6] use ResNet-34 [2] as the emotion
classifier and then adopt language models to perform explanation generation. In
this part, in order to explore the impact of different emotion classifiers on art
comprehension, we replace the emotion classifier with a more advanced CLIP
model [5]. The ablation experiment is conducted on the language model NLXGPT2 [6]. As shown in Tab. 1, compared with the ResNet-34 [2], the ACC of
the CLIP-based emotion classifier has a slight improvement, increasing by 1.6%
and 0.2% on the ArtEmis v1.0 test set and v2.0 Combined test set, respectively.
This demonstrates the positive impact and stable gain of CLIP on emotion
classification in two-stage method. However, despite this improvement, our endto-end model performs significantly better than CLIP-based classifier in the twostage method on emotion understanding. For example, the ACC and EA of our
model are +3.8% and +8.3% higher than the two-stage method using CLIP on
ArtEmis v1.0 test set. The same metrics of our method are +0.7% and +5.2%
higher on the ArtEmis v2.0 Combined test set.

Detailed Ablations of VAD Emotion Modeling

VAD vector input and emotion encoder from our model, which is called ‘w/o
VAD emotion modeling’. Another variant uses VAD vectors but does not use
emotion encoder, which is called ‘w/o emotion encoder’. A detailed description
