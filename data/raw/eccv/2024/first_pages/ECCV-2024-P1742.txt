Implementation Details

Degradation Settings. We randomly crop 256×256 image patches from the
HR images to train our RealSRT. The experiments are performed with a scaling
factor of ×4 between LR and HR images. Thus we synthesize 64×64 LR images during the training. We use the same degradation model as Real-ESRGAN
[14] for synthesizing the LR images. Following [14], we employ the secondorder degradation model for a good balance of simplicity and effectiveness. Unless otherwise specified, the two degradation processes have the same settings.
Each degradation process consists of multiple degradations including blur, downsampling, noise, and JPEG compression.
For the blur degradation, we adopt Gaussian kernels, generalized Gaussian
kernels and plateau-shaped kernels, with a probability of {0.7, 0.15, 0.15}. The
blur kernel size is randomly selected from {7, 9, ..., 21}. Blur standard deviation
σ is sampled from [0.2, 3] for the first degradation process and [0.2, 1.5] for the
second degradation process. Shape parameter β is sampled from [0.5, 4] and [1,
2] for generalized Gaussian and plateau-shaped kernels, respectively. We also use
the sinc filter with a probability of 0.1. We skip the second blur degradation with
a probability of 0.2. For the down-sampling degradation, we consider multiple
resize algorithms, including nearest-neighbor interpolation, area resize, bilinear
interpolation, and bicubic interpolation. For the noise degradation, we employ
Gaussian noises and Poisson noises with a probability of {0.5, 0.5}. The noise
sigma range and Poisson noise scale are set to [1, 30] and [0.05, 3], respectively
(and [1, 25] and [0.05, 2.5] for the second degradation process). The gray noise
probability is set to 0.4. For the JPEG compression degradation, the compression
quality factor is set to [30, 95]. We also use the sinc filter at the last step of the
synthesis with a probability of 0.8.
