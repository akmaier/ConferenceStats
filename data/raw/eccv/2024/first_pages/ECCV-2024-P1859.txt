Fig. A: An illustration of the search space adopted in our work. (a) LEFT: The architecture of the CNN consists of a convolution layer, two types of searchable cells and a
linear classifier. Normal cells can be stacked R times, and reduction cells can only be
stacked once. MIDDLE: Each cell consists of two inputs (the output and input of the
previous cell, respectively), five blocks, and one output. RIGHT: Each block has two
inputs that are separately connected to the operation, which adds up to one output.
(b) TOP: Each cell is encoded using 20 integer bits, so a total of 40 integer bits can
represent an architecture. BOTTOM: Candidate operations and the corresponding encoding scheme.
