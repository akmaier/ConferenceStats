The Proposed Dataset: I2-2000FPS

Fig. 1: Snapshots of the proposed I2-2000FPS dataset.

As shown in Figure 8(a) in the main paper, existing datasets fail to capture high-speed motion without incurring motion blur, thus failing to meet the
requirements for our problem. Therefore, to effectively simulate few-bit quanta
data with strong motion and also to push the boundaries of high-speed motion analysis, we propose and publish the first high-speed video dataset with a
temporal resolution of 2000 FPS.
We use the Chronos 1.4 high-speed CMOS sensor-based camera, with a 12.5−
75mm f/1.2 zoom lens to capture the scenes. Due to hardware limitations, we
limit the spatial resolution and relative motion range of the dataset to 512×1024
and 1 − 7 pixels/frame (∼ 1 − 30 m/s), respectively. To mitigate the influence
of read noise, we confine to capturing the scenes in ambient light and utilize
inbuilt dark calibration for hot pixel corrections. We avoid analog and digital
gains to minimize noise amplification. All these measures ensure the captured
video closely represents the scene’s intensity variations. For the dataset to closely
represent the real world we capture scenes involving objects of various shapes,
sizes, and textures. Figure 1 depicts few snapshots of objects collected in the
proposed dataset. Additionally, strong pixel displacement, as shown in Figure 2,
along with different types of motion is ensured for better generalization.
