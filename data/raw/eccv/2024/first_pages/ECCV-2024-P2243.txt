Details of Mutual Information Estimation

In Sec. 3.2, we illustrate the superiority of the frozen teacher from the perspective of the
mutual information principle. Specifically, regarding the teacher’s representation (F ), we
use detection precision (mAP) to quantify its mutual information related to target labels
(I(Y ; F )) and reconstruction loss to quantify its mutual information related to input
images (I(X; F )) [40, 44]. Here, we provide theoretical proofs for these approximation
methods.
I(X;F) Estimation. The estimation of I(X; F ) satisfies I(X; F ) = H(X)−H(X|F ) ≥
H(X) − R(X|F ) [44]. Here H(X) indicates the entropy of the input, a constant value;
R(X|F ) denotes the reconstruction error for the input image. To reconstruct input
images, we use the decoder network of UNet, as illustrated in Fig. 7. The features from
the last layer of the Transformer Encoder in DETRs are sent into the reconstruction
network. The Mean Squared Error (MSE) Loss optimizes the pixel-wise distance between
the predictions x′ and input images x:
  \mathcal {L}_{recon} = ||x' - x||_{2}. 

(8)

Given that H(X) is a constant value, we use 1−Lrecon to approximate H(X)−R(X|F ).
The training loss curves of four teacher models are depicted in Fig. 6, where the frozen
teacher significantly reduces reconstruction losses compared to the fully fine-tuned
teacher, resulting in higher values of I(X; F ). The reconstructed images of four teacher
models are visualized in Fig. 8.
