Point cloud rendering

In this section we demonstrate the rendering pipeline with synthesized camera
parameters. Given a point cloud P = (x, c) with point coordinates and colors, we
first normalize the coordinates of xyz-axis to the range of (-0.5,0.5), respectively.
We place the camera in the middle of the scene, e.g. (0,0,0), and use camera
orientation to determine the rotation of the camera. The camera orientation is
defined with polar coordinate (θ, ϕ). To effectively retrieve background features,
we split the 360° azimuths θ into eight octants. Within each octant, we detect
valid background points. Once detected, a series of azimuths and elevations are
sampled as camera orientations. With unit radius, we can define camera forward
directions as a three-dimensional forward vector. The right vector is defined as
  \textit {right} = \frac {\textit {forward} \times Z^{+}}{\| \textit {forward} \times Z^{+} \|} \, , 

(1)

which denotes the right direction from the camera center. Z + is the positive
direction of the z-axis which points to the top of the scene. Similarly, with forward
and right vectors we can derive up vector as
  \textit {up} = \frac {\textit {right} \times \textit {forward}}{ \| \textit {right} \times \textit {forward} \|} \, , 

(2)

representing the up direction of the photograph from the image center. The
complete camera pose is written as

  \bE = \begin {bmatrix} \textit {right}_x & -\textit {up}_x & \textit {forward}_x & T_x \\ \textit {right}_y & -\textit {up}_y & \textit {forward}_y & T_y \\ \textit {right}_z & -\textit {up}_z & \textit {forward}_z & T_z \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end {bmatrix} \, , 

(3)

where (Tx , Ty , Tz ) indicates camera positions. In practice, we move camera positions along z-axis to render images with more view differences. The origin of
forward is changed accordingly.
For intrinsic parameters, to accommodate the sparse nature of point clouds
we choose a relatively low resolution for rendered images. The height and width of
each image are 240 and 320, repectively. To enhance the clarity of the background
contexts, we use a 90° wide field of view for both x and y directions.
