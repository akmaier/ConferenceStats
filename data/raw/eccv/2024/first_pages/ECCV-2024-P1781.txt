Introduction

The structure of this supplementary document is as follows. Section 2 provides
a detailed derivation of polarization representations that are used in the main
paper. In Section 3, we illustrate the additional experimental results, and Section
4 demonstrates our scene representation. In Section 5, we theoretically analyze
the motivation of using polarization cues for SSS estimation. In Section 6, we
introduce the constructed synthetic dataset and real dataset. Finally, we show
the Network structure in Section 7.

Polarization representation

In this Section, we give a detailed derivation of polarization representations
that are used in the main paper. In our experiment, we used VP-TRI050S1-Q
polarization camera to capture four linearly polarized images (I0 , I45 , I90 , and
I135 ) in a single shot. The Stokes vectors (s0 , s1 , and s2 ) can be calculated by
the following equations:
  s_0 = \frac {1}{2}(I_0+I_{45}+I_{90}+I_{135}), \label {equ:unpol} 

(1)

  s_1 = I_{90} - I_{0}, 

(2)

  s_2 = 2 * I_{45} - s_0, 

(3)

where s0 = I is the unpolarized image intensity, s1 represents the horizontal
vs. vertical polarization, and s2 represents the 45-degree polarization vs. the
135-degree polarization. Then the DoP ρ and AoP ϕ can be computed by:
  \rho = \frac {\sqrt {s_1^2+s_2^2}}{s_0}, \label {equ:rho} 

(4)
