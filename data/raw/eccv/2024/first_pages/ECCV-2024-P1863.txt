Concept grounding with VLMs

We select four pre-trained VLMs in our experiments, CLIP1 [10], BLIP2 [9],
BLIP23 [8], and FLAVA4 [12], all of which can perform the image-text matching
task to generate compatibility scores between instructions and visual states.
The task instructions are generated by GPT-4 based on our carefully crafted
prompts, and visual states are rendered by the simulation environment. We
obtain checkpoints of VLMs from their official repositories, which are used to
discover key states from these unannotated demonstrations.
In Fig. 1, we observe that the VLM-grounded key states from CLIP [10]
are inaccurate and exhibit a lack of consistency across different trajectories.
We can find that the inaccuracy of VLM grounding is mainly reflected in two
aspects. First, the temporal order of the grounded key states is not aligned with
the order of the LLM-generated instructions. Specifically, we can find that the
LLM-generated manipulation instructions are in a strict chronological order for
the successful completion of a task, but VLM-grounded key states are in the
wrong chronological order. The temporal misalignment will prevent the implicit
hierarchical policy structure from making effective use of the VLM-grounded
key states. Second, the matching noise between the LLM-generated instructions
and the VLM-grounded key states is very large. Concretely, the LLM-generated
instructions and their corresponding states do not match in semantics so well.
In Fig. 1, we can even find that two completely different instructions can match
the same state, which will also hinder the following control policy training.
