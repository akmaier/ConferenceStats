Local outlier factor

The basic idea of the LOF [2] technique is to first calculate the local reachable
density of each data point based on the data density around this point, and then
use this reachable density to calculate an outlier factor for this point. The outlier
factor identifies the outlier degree of a data point. The larger the factor value,
the greater the outlier degree. Finally, the LOF [2] algorithm outputs the points
with the top-n largest degrees of outliers.
We first define the distance specific to the point p ranking the m-th as
dm (p) = d(o, p), i.e., there are m − 1 points {o′ } that satisfy the following:
  \begin {array}{l} {d}_{m}(p)>{d}(o', p) \end {array} \label {eq:num_o'}

  \begin {array}{l} {dist}_{m}(o, p)=\max ({d}_{m}(o), {d}(o,p)) \end {array} \label {eq:m_distance} 

(2)
