A

Scenario Understanding Benchmark

We propose the Scenario Understanding Benchmark, comprising the following tasks: (1) Detailed Description: This task requires VLMs to intricately
delineate the contents of driving videos, thereby assessing the model’s comprehensive comprehension of the driving video. (2) Open Vocabulary Object
Identification: VLMs are asked to respond to inquiries regarding objects appearing in the video, addressing aspects such as their color, quantity, semantic status,
and behavior. (3) Traffic Light: VLMs are required to discern the color and
change of traffic lights within the video. (4) Weather: This involves prompting
VLMs to forecast the prevailing weather conditions, recognizing that driving
considerations differ under varying weather circumstances. (5) Scene: VLMs are
required to ascertain the current scene based on the video, distinguishing between
settings such as city streets or residential streets, with an emphasis on potential
encounters with residents and pedestrians in the latter scenario. (6) Time of Day:
VLMs are tasked with perceiving the current time, and it is important to exercise
caution while driving during nighttime conditions.
Due to both the detail description and open vocabulary object identification
tasks falling under the category of open-ended VQA tasks, traditional NLP
metrics pose challenges for their evaluation. Consequently, we employ ChatGPT
to assign a relative score. To effectively assess the dynamic changes in traffic light
scenarios, we similarly utilize ChatGPT to calculate scores. The specific prompts
are detailed in Table L. For the remaining tasks, we employ an accuracy metric
to measure the overall performance of VLMs, with the accuracy for each category
within each task presented in Tables A, Table B, and Table C. The results
demonstrate that dolphins consistently exhibit superior performance across the
majority of scenarios. However, their predictive efficacy diminishes notably in
tasks associated with predictions of tunnel scenes and dawn or dusk.
