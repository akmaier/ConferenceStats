1. Introduction
1.1. Summary. In this note we state some key results about polynomial interpolation. In particular, we state the remainder formula for polynomial interpolation,
and consider the example of Chebyshev nodes of the first kind.
1.2. Motivation. Before discussing interpolation, we recall the Weierstrass approximation theorem. Let f be a real-valued function defined on a compact interval
[a, b] of R. Then, for any ε > 0, there exists a polynomial p(x) such that
|f (x) − p(x)| ≤ ε,
for all x ∈ [a, b]. This result motivates the study of numerical methods to approximate functions by polynomials; interpolation is one such method.
1.3. Basic result. Let f : [a, b] → R, and x1 < · · · < xm ∈ [a, b] be given. Then,
there exists a unique polynomial of degree at most m − 1 that interpolates f at
x1 , . . . , xm in the sense that
p(xj ) = f (xj ),
for j = 1, . . . , m. We call p the interpolating polynomial of f at x1 , . . . , xm .
