Theoretical Proof

Here, we theoretically demonstrate that our PARE-Conv is a rotation-equivariant
mapping.
Lemma 1. The linear operation flin (F) = WF is equivariant to rotations,
′
where F ∈ RC×3 is a vector-list feature and W ∈ RC ×C is a linear project
matrix.
Proof : Given a rotation R ∈ SO(3), we have:
  f_{lin}(\mathbf {F})\mathbf {R}=(\mathbf {W}\mathbf {F})\mathbf {R} = \mathbf {W}(\mathbf {F}\mathbf {R}) = f_{lin}(\mathbf {F}\mathbf {R}). 

(1)

Therefore, this linear operation is rotation-equivariant.

Therefore, the concatenation operation is rotation-equivariant.
Lemma 3. Linear combinations flc (f1 (F), f2 (F)) = λ1 f1 (F) + λ2 f2 (F) of
rotation-equivariant functions are still equivariant to rotations, where λ1 , λ2 are
coefficients and f1 (·), f2 (·) are rotation-equivariant functions.
Proof : Given a rotation R ∈ SO(3), we have:
  \begin {aligned} f_{lc}(f_1(\mathbf {F}), f_2(\mathbf {F}))\mathbf {R}&=[\lambda _1f_1(\mathbf {F}) + \lambda _2f_2(\mathbf {F})]\mathbf {R}\\&= \lambda _1f_1(\mathbf {F})\mathbf {R} + \lambda _2f_2(\mathbf {F})\mathbf {R} \\&= \lambda _1f_1(\mathbf {F} \mathbf {R}) + \lambda _2f_2(\mathbf {F}\mathbf {R}) \\&= f_{lc}(f_1(\mathbf {F}\mathbf {R}), f_2(\mathbf {F}\mathbf {R})). \end {aligned} 
(3)

Therefore, the linear combinations of rotation-equivariant functions are still
rotation-equivariant.
