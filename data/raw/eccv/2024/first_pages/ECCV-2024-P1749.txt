A.1

Gradient Attention Rollout

The Gradient Attention Rollout (GAR) [1] aims to illustrate why the attention
mechanism performs well in many scenarios in computer vision. GAR achieves
interpretability by a heat map highlighting how much areas contribute to the
output. Specifically, GAR is defined as
  \mathcal {A}_l=\begin {cases} \mathbf {A}_l(\mathbf {x})\frac {\partial \hat {y}}{ \partial \mathbf {A}_l(\mathbf {x})} \mathcal {A}_{l-1}, & \text {if } l > 0, \\ \mathbf {A}_l(\mathbf {x})\frac {\partial \hat {y}}{ \partial \mathbf {A}_l(\mathbf {x})}, & \text {if } l = 0, \end {cases} 

(A1)

where A is an abbreviation of Attn in Equation (3), and Al denotes the GAR on
the lth layer of the transformer. To generate the heat map, we assign the value
A0,i
N to the ith patch in the image, where AN represents the GAR of the last
layer, measuring the importance of each patch in the final prediction. Note that
AN is a matrix, and A0,i
N corresponds to the element at the 0-th row and i-th
column. The primary objective of GAR is to quantify the relative importance of
each input patch within the attention mechanism, and it is particularly useful
for analyzing the model behavior and explaining its decision-making process [1].
