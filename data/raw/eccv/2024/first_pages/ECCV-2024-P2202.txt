Degradation Modeling

Since real-world weather degradations are usually correlated with the scene
depth, we design a depth-aware formulation to describe the intensity of rain
streaks, fog and illumination as a function of the scene depth d based on [6, 14].
Rain streak: The rainstreak layer RS in Eq. (1) is composed of two parts, i.e., a
real-world visual intensity map tr of rain streaks based on the scene depth d, and
an intensity map of uniformly-distributed rain streaks Rmap ∈ [0, 1]. Therefore,
the rainstreak layer RS can be formulated as:
  RS=R_{\text {map}}~*~t_r. 

(10)

In particular, ∗ denotes the pixel-wise multiplication, while tr is modeled as:
  t_r = e^{-\max (d_1,d)}. 

(11)

Herein, d1 = 2f r, where f signifies the focal length and r denotes the radius
of individual raindrops [4]. (1) In particular, when scene objects are located in
close proximity to the camera (d ≤ d1 ), the corresponding image region will
predominantly exhibit rain streaks with minimal fog. (2) Notably, as d increases
from d1 to d2 (d ≥ d1 ), the intensity of rain streaks will decrease, since the
intensity of fog increases. (3) Furthermore, when scene objects are located at a
significant distance from the camera (d ≫ d1 ), the associated region will mainly
exhibit fog with minimal rain streaks, as tr approaches 0 with increasing d.
Fog: The fog intensity A increases continuously with depth d as:
  A=1-e^{-d}. 

(12)

If we only consider the impact of fog, the corrupted image can be formulated as:
  I_{foggy} = I_{clean}(1-A)+A_0A, 

(13)

where If oggy is the observed foggy image, Iclean is the original clean image. A0
is the atmospheric light which is assumed to be a global constant following [14].
