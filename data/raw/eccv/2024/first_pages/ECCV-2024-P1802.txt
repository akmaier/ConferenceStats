Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model
Given a data sample z ∼ q(z), at each time step t = 1, 2, . . . , T , DDPMs [30, 83]
gradually transform z = z0 into zT by adding noise in a Markovian diffusion
process defined by
  q \left ( \rv {z}_{1:T} \vert \rv {z}_0 \right ) &:= \prod _{t=1}^T q ( \rv {z}_t \vert \rv {z}_{t-1} );\\ \label {eq:q-zt-given-zt-1} q(\rv {z}_t \vert \rv {z}_{t-1}) &:= \normaldist {\sqrt {1 -\beta _t} \rv {z}_{t-1}}{\beta _t I},
(19)
where N (µ, σ) denotes multivariate Gaussian distribution with mean µ and
variance σ, and {βt }t is a pre-determined variance schedule controlling the rate
of diffusion. If T is sufficiently large (1000 steps in practice), p(zT ) approaches
the standard Gaussian.
DDPMs learn an inverse Markovian process called reverse process parametrized
by ζ that inverts the forward diffusion, transforming the standard Gaussian noise
zT back to a data sample:
  \label {eq:markov} p_\rv {\zeta }(\rv {z}_{0:T}) &:= p(\rv {z}_T) \prod _{t=1}^T p_\rv {\zeta }(\rv {z}_{t-1} \vert \rv {z}_t);\\ \label {eq:p-zt-1-given-zt} p_\rv {\zeta }(\rv {z}_{t-1} \vert \rv {z}_t) &:= \normaldist {\mu _\rv {\zeta }(\rv {z}_t, t)}{\sigma _t^2 I},
(21)
where µζ (zt , t) represents the predicted mean for the Gaussian distribution at
time step t and {σt }t is the variance schedule.
DDPMs are trained by maximizing the variational lower bound of log-likelihood
of the data z0 under q(z0 ):
  \label {eq:variational-lower-bound} \mathbb {E}_{q(\rv {z}_0)} \left [ \log p_\rv {\zeta }(\rv {z}_0) \right ] \geq \mathbb {E}_{q(\rv {z}_{0:T})} \left [ \log \frac {p_\rv {\zeta }(\rv {z}_{0:T})}{q(\rv {z}_{1:T} \vert \rv {z}_0)} \right ].

(22)

Expanding Eq. (22) with Eq. (20) and noticing that p(zT ) and q(z1:T |z0 ) are
constant with respect to ζ, we obtain our objective function to maximize:
  \label {eq:pre-objective} \mathbb {E}_{q(\rv {z}_0),q(\rv {z}_{1:T} \vert \rv {z}_0)} \left [ \sum _{t=1}^T \log p_\rv {\zeta } ( \rv {z}_{t-1} \vert \rv {z}_t) \right ].

(23)
