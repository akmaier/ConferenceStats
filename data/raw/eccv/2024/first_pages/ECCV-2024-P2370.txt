The Jacobian T1 of a convolution with a stride s = 1 can be expressed as a
summation of block doubly Toeplitz matrices:
 T_1= \sum _{k=-h_1}^{h_2}\sum _{p=-w_1}^{w_2}J_k \otimes J_p \otimes K_{:, :, k, p}.
We define a “strided Toeplitz matrix” as a Toeplitz matrix where each row is
shifted by s > 1, resulting in a shape of ns × n. For example, when n = 6 and
s = 2, the strided Toeplitz matrix takes the form:

 A=\begin {bmatrix} a & b & c & d & 0 & 0\\ e & f & a & b & c & d\\ 0 & 0 & e & f & a & b \end {bmatrix}.

Slicing this matrix by selecting columns at intervals determined by the stride s,
such as columns (0, s, 2 · s, . . . ( ns − 1) · s), results in a standard Toeplitz matrix.
We can write this property as
 A=\sum _{j=0}^{s-1}A_{j}(I_{\frac {n}{s}} \otimes e_j^T).
