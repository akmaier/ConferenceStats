A

Additional Implementation Details

Additional Training Details. In the pre-training phase on Ref-COCO [10], we initiate
with distinct learning rates for different components: 2.5e-6 for the text encoder, and
2.5e-5 for the remaining parts of the model. This stage spans 12 epochs, focusing on
single-frame inputs. The learning rates undergo a reduction by a factor of 10 at the 8-th
and 10-th epochs to refine the training process. In our main training phase, we adjust
the training length to 6 or 9 epochs, depending on whether we use pre-training or not.
The text encoder is kept frozen during training. Initial learning rates are set at 5e-5 for
the whole model. For a 9-epoch training setting, learning rates are decreased by a factor
of 10 at the 6-th and 8-th epochs. In contrast, for a 6-epoch schedule, reductions occur
at the 3-rd and 5-th epochs.
Additional Training Loss Details. To ensure a fair comparison, we utilize the identical
training losses and weights that are mentioned in [1, 5, 8]. Therefore, our segmentation
consists of three sub-heads: bounding box head, classification head and mask head.
These correspond to three types of outputs: bounding boxes B, classification scores S,
and segmentation masks, which include both a low-resolution Mo and a refined highresolution M. The overall training loss equation used to optimize these outputs is
  \mathcal {L}_{\text {train }}=\lambda _{\mathcal {M}_o} \mathcal {L}_{\mathcal {M}_o}+\lambda _{\mathcal {M}} \mathcal {L}_{\mathcal {M}}+\lambda _{\mathcal {B}} \mathcal {L}_{\mathcal {B}}+\lambda _{\mathcal {S}} \mathcal {L}_{\mathcal {S}}, 

(1)

where L represents the different loss components and λ denotes their respective weights.
Specifically, LM and LMo are mask losses. LB is bounding box loss and LS is classification loss. Specifically, we use Dice loss [3] and Focal loss [4] for masks {Mo , M},
Focal loss [4] for confidence scores S, and L1 and GIoU [6] loss for bounding boxes B.
Additional Matching Loss Details. Our approach to instance matching is in line with
the recent transformer-based methods [1, 5, 7–9]. In particular, we adopt the Hungarian
algorithm [2] to select the most suitable match between the Q = 5 instance queries and
the ground truth. For this purpose, final masks M, bounding boxes B and classification
scores S are used to compute the matching loss Lmatch for each query and Hungarian
algorithm is used to find the best match that has the minimum loss. Lmatch lies in three
parts, which can be formulated as
  \mathcal {L}_{\text {match }}=\lambda _{\mathcal {M}} \mathcal {L}_{\mathcal {M}}+\lambda _{\mathcal {B}} \mathcal {L}_{\mathcal {B}}+\lambda _{\mathcal {S}} \mathcal {L}_{\mathcal {S}}.
