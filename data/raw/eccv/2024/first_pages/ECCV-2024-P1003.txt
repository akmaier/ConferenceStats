This Supplementary Material is structured as follows: in Section 1 we provide
more details about the datasets, the data rendering pipelines and ground-truth
processing; in Section 2 we present full results for the PartNetE few-shot and
zero-shot experiments in the main text; in Section 3 we perform additional ablation studies for our method; in Section 4, we provide implementation details
of our approach; in Section 5, we demonstrate the effectiveness of the semantic
correspondence matching process; in Section 6 we discuss about the limitation
of our work and potential future directions; in Section 7, we present additional
qualitative results.

Data

1.1

Datasets

PartNet-Ensembled [7]. This dataset combines objects from the PartNetMobility dataset [17] for few-shot and test splits, along with additional training
objects from PartNet [8]. In our study, we exclusively use the few-shot and test
splits. The few-shot split encompasses 8 objects per category across all 45 categories, while the test split spans from 6 to 338 objects per category, having
1906 objects in total. PartNetE consists of 106 parts, ranging from basic components like chair backs to more fine-grained parts such as scissors screws. Note
that compared to the level-3 annotations of PartNet [8], PartNetE exhibits a
coarser granularity and consists of 28 additional categories from PartNet Mobility dataset [17].
PartNet [8]. This dataset consists of annotations at various levels of granularity,
spanning from level-1 to level-3, where level-3 represents the most fine-grained
part annotations, including labels such as “back_frame_vertical_bar" for chairs.
We perform experiments on 10 object categories with level-3 part annotations.
As mentioned in the main text, we use a subset of PartNet that overlaps with
ShapeNet [2].
