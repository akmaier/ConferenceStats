Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion

Hilbert-Schmidt Independence Criterion (HSIC) [5] is a widely used measure of
independence. It is defined based on the cross-covariance operator between the
distributions in the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). Let X ∼ PX :
Ω → X and Y ∼ PY : Ω → Y be the two random variables in the non-empty
set X and Y , respectively. They are sampled from PX and PY distribution of
the sample space Ω. The formulation of HSIC is defined as:
  \small \begin {aligned} \operatorname {HSIC} & \left (\mathbb {P}_{X Y}, \mathcal {H}, \mathcal {G}\right ) =\left \|C_{X Y}\right \|^2 \\ = & \mathbb {E}_{X Y X^{\prime } Y^{\prime }}\left [k_X\left (X, X^{\prime }\right ) k_{Y^{\prime }}\left (Y, Y^{\prime }\right )\right ] \\ +&\mathbb {E}_{X X^{\prime }}\left [k_X\left (X, X^{\prime }\right )\right ] \mathbb {E}_{Y^{\prime }}\left [k_Y\left (Y, Y^{\prime }\right )\right ] \\ -&2 \mathbb {E}_{X Y}\left [\mathbb {E}_{X^{\prime }}\left [k_X\left (X, X^{\prime }\right )\right ] \mathbb {E}_{Y^{\prime }}\left [k_Y\left (Y, Y^{\prime }\right )\right ]\right ], \end {aligned}

where kX and kY are kernel functions, H and G are Hilbert spaces. EXY is the
expectation over X and Y.
