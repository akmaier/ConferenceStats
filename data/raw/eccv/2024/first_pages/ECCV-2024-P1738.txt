As mentioned in the main paper, we employ VRT across various tasks to
assess its efficacy. Consequently, our primary focus lies in illustrating how the
acquired multi-view relationship representations from VRT can contribute to
diverse tasks, such as visual relationship detection, novel view synthesis, pedestrian intention estimation, and visual grounding. This section will delve into the
detailed frameworks for integrating VRT as a plug-in module for the aforementioned tasks, respectively.
We provide more analysis and experiments in the supplementary materials.
The contents include (1) frameworks for employing the VRT in Synsin [12], pixelnerf [15], PIE [9], and ReferIt3d [1]; (2) mapping of relationships between the
VRDFormer [16] and the Graph-RCNN [14]; (3) results analysis compared with
the VRDFormer [16]; (4) comparison with large language model; (5) explanation
of equivariance of the message-passing; (6) additional experimental results. As
claimed in the main paper, our goal in adapting the VRT to different tasks is to
validate the effectiveness of the learned multi-view understanding of the relationship representations, rather than beating the state-of-the-art visual relationship
detection, graph neural network (GNN), novel view synthesis, pedestrian intention estimation, pedestrian intention estimation, and visual grounding methods.
More sophisticated networks, as long as end-to-end trainable, can be readily
adopted in the proposed approach to achieve potentially better results.
