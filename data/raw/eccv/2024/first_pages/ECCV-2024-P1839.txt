Easy & Hard Examples

In our analysis in Sec. 1, we categorize examples from the G-Ref UMD test set
into ‘easy’ and ‘hard’ samples to illustrate the varying levels of challenge present
within the same dataset.
The ‘Easy’ samples in Fig. I feature scenes with a single object in a category,
making them straightforward to identify. For example, the ‘airplane’ in the second image of the second row is the only object in its category, clearly aligning
with the referring text. In other scenes like the ‘SUV’ (row 1, column 2) and
the ‘bench’ (row 4, column 3), despite multiple objects in view, the target object
stands out as the only one in their respective categories. These samples highlight
instances within the dataset where the target can be readily determined by the
subject alone.
On the other hand, ‘hard’ samples feature multiple objects from the same
category, requiring a nuanced understanding of context to pinpoint the target.
For instance, in Fig. II, to find the right ‘giraﬀe’ in the first row’s third image,
one must discern its position relative to the ‘other two’ giraﬀes. In addition to
positional cues, some instances demand comprehension of descriptive phrases or
modifiers to correctly identify objects. In the second row’s first image, finding
the ‘flower vase’ depends on grasping the word ‘narrow’, and locating ‘a man’ in
the third row’s third image necessitates understanding the action ‘working on a
computer’.
These examples in the Fig. I and Fig. II demonstrate how the complexity of
a scene aﬀects the diﬃculty of object identification.

Implementation Details

We cross-validate within [0.2, 0.8] and choose 0.6 as the default. We empirically
set (⌧ , K) to (0.75, 200) for G-Ref and (0.85, 800) for RefCOCO(+) (see Sec. 4.5).
For the RIS models, we employ Swin Transformer Base [33] and BERT [9] for the
visual and text backbone, respectively. In the case of CRIS, we use ResNet50 [14]
and Transformer [44] backbones. We use the AdamW [49] optimizer with learning
rates of 10 5 for ReLA, 5 · 10 5 for VPD and LAVT, and 10 4 for CGFormer
and CRIS. The training epochs remain consistent with the original papers. Batch
size is set to 32 for LAVT and VPD, and 64 for CGFormer and CRIS. Unless
noted above, we follow the settings from the original papers.
