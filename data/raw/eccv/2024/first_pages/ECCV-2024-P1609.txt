Assessing Stable Diffusion’s Prior Knowledge of Logos

In this section, we demonstrate that Stable Diffusion [9] does not have adequate
prior knowledge of logos, and thus customizing logos is more difficult than commonplace items. In Fig. 1, we visualize the logo references and their synthesized
counterparts by Stable Diffusion using text prompts. We can see that Stable Diffusion is not able to synthesize both Chinese and English texts. The generated
images only contain gibberish. For the Vans logo, Stable Diffusion erroneously
generates an actual shoe. For the Meta logo, Stable Diffusion generates something completely unrelated. The Nike logo is less complex, but it is still difficult
to synthesize the one with the “Nike” text. All these examples demonstrate the
challenge of accurately generating logos using Stable Diffusion, not to mention
contextualizing them appropriately in diverse scenarios. Therefore, the evident
lack of prior knowledge about logos in the Stable Diffusion highlights the complexities and challenges inherent in customizing logos and further emphasizes
the necessity of our proposed method.
