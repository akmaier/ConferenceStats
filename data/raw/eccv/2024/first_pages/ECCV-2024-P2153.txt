Additional Details of Dropout Strategy

We iteratively drop out less essential primitives and convex shapes. While the main
paper (Section 4) outlines the primitive dropout operations, this section provides further
details on the convex shape dropout method. Specifically, the weight vector w values
are initially set to 1. To drop out the cth convex shape, we set the cth entry of w to zero.
During convex shape dropout, Equation (1) of a∗ in the main paper is revised to the
following equation to remove the effects of unused convex shapes:
  \nv {a}^*_i = \min _{1 \leq c \leq C} \left (\nv {O}_{i,c} + (1-\nv {w}_c)*\theta \right ) \hspace {0.35cm} \begin {cases} = 0 & \text {in,} \\ > 0 & \text {out.} \end {cases} \label {label_a_*_2} 

(1)

We set θ as 100 in all experiments. We also provide the pseudo-codes of the complete algorithm in Algorithm 1. We use the same notations to define all the variables as
in the main paper.

Mesh Construction

During inference, we construct a CAD-like mesh as the final shape from the estimated
primitive parameter matrix P and the estimated selection matrix T. In particular, we
first get the mesh for each primitive by applying the marching cubes algorithm on
the signed distance field produced by the quadric equation of that primitive. Considering the relatively simple geometry of each quadric primitive, we set the resolution as
64 to achieve a low-polygon mesh. We then perform the intersection operations over
primitives from the same convex shape according to T. Finally, the convex shapes are
unioned into the final shape mesh. The CSG operations utilized to assemble the meshes
are from the PyMesh library [2]. Fig. 1 shows the CAD mesh construction example of
one shape.
