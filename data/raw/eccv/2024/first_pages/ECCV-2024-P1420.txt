Proof of Spherical Motion

In Section 3.2 of the paper, we propose that the relative motion between the
calibration target and the camera conforms to the spherical motion model. This
section gives more details of the proof. By arbitrarily selecting a pair of planar
points Pi and Pj on the target and transforming them, we can have the following
equation from the angle invariance:
  \angle \left (\mathbf {P}_i , \mathbf {P}_j \right ) = \angle \left (\mathbf {R}\left (\mathbf {P}_i+\mathbf {t}\right ), \mathbf {R}\left (\mathbf {P}_j +\mathbf {t}\right )\right ) \label {eq:RPt1} 

(1)

where R is rotation matrix and t is translation vector. According to the rotation
invariance [6], a pair of 3D points are jointly rotated about the origin, their angular distance remains unchanged. The angular distance means the angle between
two vectors from the origin to two points. Therefore, we have:
  \angle (\mathbf {P}_i, \mathbf {P}_j)) = \angle (\mathbf {P}_i+\mathbf {t}, \mathbf {P}_j +\mathbf {t}). \label {eq:RPt2} 

(2)

To make Eq. (2) hold for any pair of points, the translation must be 03×1 vector
(t = [0, 0, 0]T ). We select several special point pairs to support our proof. Our
proof is similar to the proof by contradiction. Angle invariance holds for any
point pair on the target, so it must hold for specific pairs. We prove that for
specific point pairs, the condition for angle invariance is t = 0. If t is non-zero,
angle invariance is not maintained. The detailed proof is given as follows.
Without loss of generality, we establish a coordinate system with the camera
optical center as the origin. The z-axis is perpendicular to the plane of the target,
and the xy-plane is parallel to the target. We assume that the distance from the
origin to the target is r(r > 0).
