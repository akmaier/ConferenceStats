Characterisation of Symmetries

While in the main paper we represent symmetry as a general coordinate transformation, or rather as a symmetry transformation in a canonical coordinate
frame, that is, Tc−1 STc , here we provide a taxonomy of some specific symmetry
types and how they can be implemented. Some terminology and formulae are
adapted from Rosen [8] and Thrun & Wegbreit [10]. Note that here we only consider point transformations; direction vectors are invariant to translation and so
only undergo the rotation or scaling parts of the transformation.
1. Reflection symmetry: whereby a point is mapped to another point (a
bijection) on the opposite side of a plane, line or point.
(a) Planar reflection: for a plane defined by a normal vector n and scalar
distance from the origin ∆, a point x is mapped to
