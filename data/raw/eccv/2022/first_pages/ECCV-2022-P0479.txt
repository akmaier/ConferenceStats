Connection with kernel methods

This section provides a connection between LDU (section 3.2) and kernel ridge regression. Let us consider the generic training of a kernel ridge regressor model [6]
f on the training set {(xi , yi )}ni=1 , where n is the number of training samples.
Let us denote by k the kernel. The Representer
PnTheorem [6] states that the solution to this problem is of the form f(x) = i=1 αi k(xi , x), where αi are the
parameters to optimize.
Therefore, one should evaluate the kernel centered in each training sample.
If one relates the proposed LDU model to kernel ridge regression, the main
distinction is that we do not evaluate it across the entire training
Pm set, but across
a smaller set composed of the prototypes. Hence f(x) = i=1 αi k (pi , hω (xi ))
with k (·, ·) = exp(−Sc (·, ·)), which is a composition of kernel positive definite
functions. In contrast with DUQ [7] and similarly to SNGP [3] , we approximate
the kernel ridge regression based on the prototype set which allows us to simplify
the training. However for SNGP, the authors choose to approximate the kernel
differently, by relying on the random projection trick [5].
