Architecture Details

In this section, we elaborate the details of the proposed architectures in Sec. 1.1
and the comparison methods in the few-shot experiments in Sec. 1.2.
1.1

The proposed architectures

For CLIP, we provide three variants: X-CLIP-B/32, X-CLIP-B/16 and X-CLIPL/14. In detail, there are three parts in our framework: a cross-frame communication transformer followed by a multi-frame integration transformer and a text
encoder. X-CLIP-B/32 adopts ViT-B/32 (Lc =12, Nh =12, d=768, p=32) as parts
of the cross-frame communication transformer, X-CLIP-B/16 uses ViT-B/16
(Lc =12, Nh =12, d=768, p=16), while X-CLIP-L/14 employs ViT-L/14 (Lc =24,
Nh =12, d=1, 024, p=14), where Lc denotes the layers, Nh refers to the number
of attention heads, d represents the embedding dimension and p is the patch size.
We use a simple 1-layer multi-frame integration transformer for all three X-CLIP
variants (Lm =1, Nh =8 for X-CLIP-B while Nh =12 for X-CLIP-L). The text
encoder is the same as in CLIP [10]. For Florence, we replace the cross-frame
communication transformer with the pretrained CoSwin-H [13] visual encoder.
We stack a 4-layer multi-frame integration transformer on top of CoSwin-H. The
text encoder is the same as in Florence [13]. In both X-CLIP and X-Florence,
the number of the video-specific prompting blocks is set to 2.
