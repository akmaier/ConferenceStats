Proof of Approximate KL-Divergence

Formally, the KL-Divergence [4] can be defined as:
  D_{KL}(p_\theta (\mathbf {y}|\mathbf {x})||p_{\theta +\Delta \theta }(\mathbf {y}|\mathbf {x})=\mathbb {E}_{\left (\mathbf {y},\mathbf {x}\right )\sim D}\left [\log p_\theta \left (\mathbf {y}\middle |\mathbf {x}\right )-\log p_{\theta +\Delta \theta }\left (\mathbf {y}\middle |\mathbf {x}\right )\right ]. \label {eq:1} 

(1)

With the second Taylor Expansion of log pθ+∆θ (y|x) at θ, we can get:
  \label {eq:taylor} \log p_{\theta +\Delta \theta }\left (\mathbf {y}\middle |\mathbf {x}\right )\approx \log p_{\theta }\left (\mathbf {y}\middle |\mathbf {x}\right )+\Delta \theta ^\top \frac {\partial \log p_{\theta }\left (\mathbf {y}\middle |\mathbf {x}\right )}{\partial \theta }+\frac {1}{2}\Delta \theta ^\top \frac {\partial ^2 \log p_{\theta }\left (\mathbf {y}\middle |\mathbf {x}\right )}{\partial \theta ^2}\Delta \theta . 
(2)
Then, substitute Eq. (2) into Eq. (1) and we get the approximate KL-Divergence:
