Supplementary Experiments

A.1

Different Similarity indexes to Calculate the Similarity Matrix

In this subsection, we replace cosine similarity with pearson correlation coefficient to calculate the similarity matrix. We can observe from Fig. B1 that the
modularity curves are almost the same as those in the manuscript, demonstrating
that various similarity indexes can be utilized to obtain the modularity.
A.2

Modularity curves of Randomly Initialized Models

Having observed the upward trend of modularity emerging in pre-trained models,
we next study whether this phenomenon exists in randomly initialized models.
Hence, we conduct experiments with VGG16 and ResNet18 on CIFAR-10. In
Fig. B2, we show the modularity curves of randomly initialized models. Since
the randomly initialized models can not learn useful feature representations,
the connections among samples at each layer are completely random. Thus,
the modularity nearly maintains constant in the randomly initialized models
compared to pre-trained models.
