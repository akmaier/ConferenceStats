In this supplementary material, we clarify the network architecture in Sec. 1.
And we will introduce the training loss of RRSR in Sec. 2. Then, we discuss
several reference-aware feature selection mechanisms in Sec. 3. Additionally, we
conduct a detailed ablation study on our reciprocal target-reference reconstruction (RTRR) in Sec. 4. We also describe the computational efficiency of our
RRSR in Sec. 5. Finally, we show more qualitative results in Sec. 6.

1

Network Architecture

In our framework, we improve C 2 -Matching by replacing its feature alignment
procedure by our progressive FAS, which contains our newly added RASs and
MDCNs. Meanwhile, the CCN(Contrastive Correspondence Network), CE(Content
Extractor), VGG, etc remain unchanged. We further reduce the number of resblocks to 5 to save computation budget for RASs and MDCNs. The number of
learnable filters in each FAS module is 16.

Loss Functions

Our overall objective is formulated as
  \label {equa:all_loss} \mathcal {L} = \lambda _{rec}\mathcal {L}_{rec} + \lambda _{RTRR}\mathcal {L}_{RTRR} + \lambda _{per}\mathcal {L}_{per} + \lambda _{adv}\mathcal {L}_{adv}\,. 

(1)

Reconstruction loss. We adopt ℓ1 -norm to calculate loss between the groundtruth image XHR and the output image XSR , as
  \label {equa:rec_loss} \mathcal {L}_{rec} = \|X_{HR} - X_{SR}\|_{1}, ~~~~X_{SR} = RefSR(X_{LR}, Y_{HR}) \,. 

(2)

Reciprocal loss. As elaborated in the main body, we derive the loss function
for reciprocal learning:
  \label {equa:RTRR_loss} \mathcal {L}_{RTRR} = \|Y_{HR}^{\!\mathcal {P}} - Y_{SR}^{\!\mathcal {P}}\|_{1}, ~~~~Y_{SR}^{\!\mathcal {P}} = RefSR(Y_{LR}^{\!\mathcal {P}}, X_{SR}) \,,
