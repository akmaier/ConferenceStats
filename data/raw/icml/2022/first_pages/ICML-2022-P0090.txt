1. Introduction
Counterfactual reasoning is at the very heart of causal inference (Pearl, 2009a;b; Morgan & Winship, 2015). Counterfactuals are answers to “what would have happened if”
questions: if a person has been denied a loan, would their
application have been approved if their annual income were
$20k higher? In explainable ML, there is a growing interest
in the use of counterfactual explanations (Wachter et al.,
2017) to understand the predictions of black box models:
given a model f and an instance x? , we would like to efficiently determine how a few features of x? can be changed
to obtain a similar instance x0 for which f (x0 ) 6= f (x? ).
