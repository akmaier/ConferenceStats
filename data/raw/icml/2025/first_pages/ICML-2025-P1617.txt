Bridging contrastive language-image pre-training
(CLIP) to video action recognition has attracted
growing interest. Human actions are inherently
rich in spatial and temporal contexts, involving
dynamic interactions among people, objects, and
the environment. Accurately recognizing actions
requires effectively capturing these fine-grained
elements and modeling their relationships with
language. However, most existing methods rely
on cosine similarity–practically equivalent to the
Pearson correlation coefficient–between global
tokens for video-language alignment. As a result, they have limited capacity to model complex dependencies and tend to overlook local tokens that encode critical spatio-temporal cues. To
overcome these limitations, we propose BDCCLIP, a novel framework that leverages Brownian Distance Covariance (BDC) to align visual
and textual representations. Our method can
capture complex relationships–both linear and
nonlinear–between all visual and textual tokens,
enabling fine-grained modeling in space, time,
and language. BDC-CLIP achieves state-of-theart performance across zero-shot, few-shot, baseto-novel, and fully supervised action recognition
settings, demonstrating its effectiveness and broad
applicability.
