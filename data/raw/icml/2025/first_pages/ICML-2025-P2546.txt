Training safe LLMs remains a critical challenge.
The most widely used method, Refusal Training
(RT), struggles to generalize against various Outof-Distribution (OOD) jailbreaking attacks. Although various advanced methods have been proposed to address this issue, we instead question
whether OOD attacks inherently surpass the capability of vanilla RT. Evaluations using Best-of-N
(BoN) reveal significant safety improvements as
N increases, indicating models possess adequate
latent safety knowledge but RT fails to consistently elicit it under OOD scenarios. Further domain adaptation analysis reveals that direct RT
causes reliance on superficial shortcuts, resulting in non-generalizable representation mappings.
Inspired by our findings, we propose training
model to perform safety reasoning for each query.
Specifically, we synthesize reasoning supervision
aligned with specified guidelines that reflect diverse perspectives on safety knowledge. This encourages model to engage in deeper reasoning, explicitly eliciting and utilizing latent safety knowledge for each query. Extensive experiments show
that our method significantly improves model generalization against OOD attacks.

To address this issue, various safety training methods have
been proposed, such as using regularized training objective (Qi et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024b) or intervening in
the model’s internal representations (Sheshadri et al., 2024;
Zou et al., 2024a). While these prior studies offer valuable
insights, we complement this research by investigating a
critical yet underexplored question: Do OOD attacks truly
exceed the inherent capability of RT models, or do models simply fail to leverage their pretrained safety-related
knowledge when confronted with OOD scenarios?
To explore this question, we move beyond the traditional
greedy decoding approach for evaluating attack success
rates (ASR), and instead adopt a more comprehensive evaluation method—Best-of-N (BoN) sampling (Stiennon et al.,
2020; Gao et al., 2023). Our experiments demonstrate a
significant reduction in ASR on OOD attacks as N increases
(Figure 1), underscoring that models indeed possess sufficient latent safety knowledge. However, RT training fails
to consistently elicit this knowledge when addressing OOD
cases. Further analysis through the lens of domain adaptation (Ben-David et al., 2006) reveals that reliance on direct refusal—a simplistic and easily learnable supervision
signal—leads models to depend on superficial shortcuts,
resulting in non-generalizable representation mappings (Figure 2). This ultimately explains their poor generalization
performance on OOD attacks. In short, the under-specified
nature (Amodei et al., 2016; D’Amour et al., 2022; Weng,
2024) of the simple refusal supervision fails to encourage
generalizable safety alignment, limiting the model’s ability
to handle diverse and complex OOD scenarios.
