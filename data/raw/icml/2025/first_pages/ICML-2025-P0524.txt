To address this challenge, researchers have increasingly
turned to parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques,
which update only a small subset of parameters while keeping most of the backbone frozen, enabling efficient and scalable adaptation (Houlsby et al., 2019; Lester et al., 2021;
Hu et al., 2021). Among these approaches, the LLaMAAdapter (Zhang et al., 2024) has emerged as a specialized
PEFT method for transforming LLaMA into an instructionfollowing model, demonstrating strong performance across
multiple benchmarks. Beyond adaptation prompts, its key
innovation lies in the introduction of zero-initialized attention with zero gating, which facilitates the seamless integration of new instructional prompts while preserving the
model’s pre-existing knowledge. However, despite its empirical success, the theoretical foundations of zero-initialized
attention remain largely unexplored, limiting a deeper understanding of its underlying mechanisms and potential for
further advancements.
