and the Seoul AI Safety Summit (United Kingdom Department for Science, Innovation & Technology, 2024), is that

central to effective AI governance. Several benchmarks
exist that aim to evaluate models’ capabilities of performing various kinds of tasks, such as programming, computer
use, and question-answering (Tian et al., 2024; Rein et al.,
2023; Mialon et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024; Huang et al.,
2024; Jansen et al., 2024; LeGris et al., 2024). However,
few existing evaluations attempt to measure autonomous AI
R&D capabilities, particularly the kind of research required
to develop frontier AI systems.

Frontier AI safety policies highlight automation
of AI research and development (R&D) by AI
agents as an important capability to anticipate.
However, there exist few evaluations for AI R&D
capabilities, and none that are highly realistic and
have a direct comparison to human performance.
We introduce RE-Bench (Research Engineering
Benchmark, V1), which consists of 7 challenging, open-ended ML research engineering environments and data from 71 8-hour attempts by
61 distinct human experts. We confirm that our
experts make progress in the environments given
8 hours, with 82% of expert attempts achieving
a non-zero score and 24% matching or exceeding our strong reference solutions. We compare
humans to several public frontier models through
best-of-k with varying time budgets and agent designs, and find that the best AI agents achieve a
score 4× higher than human experts when both
are given a total time budget of 2 hours per environment. However, humans currently display
better returns to increasing time budgets, narrowly
exceeding the top AI agent scores given an 8-hour
budget, and achieving 2× the score of the top AI
agent when both are given 32 total hours (across
different attempts).

The concern is that AI agents automating AI R&D could
drastically increase the amount of skilled research labor
available to AI developers, which would likely accelerate
further progress, potentially leading to a runaway feedback
loop of accelerating progress (Davidson, 2023; Erdil et al.,
2024; Erdil and Besiroglu, 2024). The consequences of
rapidly advancing the pace of scientific discoveries could be
dramatic and enable many positive outcomes, but some risks
from such acceleration could include capability progress
outpacing the development of safety measures (OpenAI,
2023) and model weight theft leading to proliferation of
highly capable AI systems, amplifying other risks (Google
