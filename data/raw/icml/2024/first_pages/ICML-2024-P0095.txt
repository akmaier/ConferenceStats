We typically solve the finite-sum optimization Problem (1)
by incremental first-order oracle (IFO) methods (Agarwal &
Bottou, 2015), which can access the pair {fi (x), ∇fi (x)}
for given x ∈ Rd and i ∈ [n]. This class of methods can
leverage the structure of the objective (Johnson & Zhang,
2013; Defazio et al., 2014; Schmidt et al., 2017; Zhang et al.,
2013) to iterate with one or mini-batch individual gradient,
which is more efficient than the iteration with the full-batch
gradient. IFO methods have received a lot of attention in
recent years. For example, the stochastic variance reduced
gradient (SVRG) methods with negative momentum (Qian
et al., 2021; Allen-Zhu, 2017; Kovalev et al., 2020a; Woodworth & Srebro, 2016; Agarwal & Bottou, 2015) achieve
the (near) optimal IFO complexity for convex optimization;
the stochastic recursive gradient methods (Nguyen et al.,
2017; Fang et al., 2018; Li et al., 2021; Pham et al., 2020;
Wang et al., 2019; Zhou & Gu, 2019; Carmon et al., 2020)
achieve the optimal IFO complexity for finding approximate stationary points in general nonconvex optimization.
