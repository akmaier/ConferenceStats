With advances in computer vision, non-contact measurement techniques are increasingly developed for automatic
and precise anthropometric measurement. Current visionbased methods fall into two categories: image-based [24,
32, 48] and 3D model-based approaches [13, 28, 29, 52, 55].
Image-based methods use single [24], multiple RGB images
[48], or video sequences [32] to define measurement landmarks and extract body dimensions. These methods are accessible, cost-effective, and convenient, making them ideal
for consumer applications such as virtual fitting rooms, fitness tracking, and basic health assessments. However, 2Dbased approaches face several limitations: (i) Lack of inherent 3D information: while multi-view images improve
depth estimation, they increase computational complexity
and require robust algorithms for large-scale data processing; (ii) Environmental sensitivity: performance is affected
by lighting conditions, camera resolution, and lens distortion [62]; (iii) Clothing interference: loose or thick garments obscure body contours, leading to inaccurate measurements. Due to these limitations, image-based methods are unsuitable for applications requiring high precision,
such as bespoke clothing design, custom prosthetics, and
ergonomic equipment development.
3D scans provide precise geometric information, enabling
more accurate and intuitive anthropometric measurements.
However, existing scan-based methods typically require a
complete 3D body scan with the subject undressed or minimally clothed in a canonical standing posture (e.g., A-pose
or T-pose) [13, 52], which is often impractical. Additionally, these methods are sensitive to scan impairments, such
as noise and missing regions [39]. The introduction of
parametric human models, such as SCAPE [1] and SMPL
[37], has led to an alternative approach: fitting parametric
models to raw body scans in a canonical posture instead
of measuring the subject directly. This approach offers a

While automatic anthropometric measurement extraction
has witnessed growth in recent years, effective, non-contact,
and precise measurement methods for dressed humans in
arbitrary poses are still lacking, limiting the widespread application of this technology. The occlusion caused by clothing and the adverse influence of posture on body shape significantly increase the complexity of this task. Additionally,
current methods often assume the availability of a complete
3D body mesh in a canonical pose (e.g., ”A” or ”T” pose),
which is not always the case in practice. To address these
challenges, we propose MeasureXpert, a novel learningbased model that requires only two unregistered, partial,
and dressed body scans as input, and accommodates entirely independent and arbitrary poses for each scan. MeasureXpert computes a comprehensive representation of the
naked body shape by synergistically fusing features from the
front- and back-view partial point clouds. The comprehensive representation obtained is mapped onto a 3D undressed
body shape space, assuming a canonical posture and incorporating predefined measurement landmarks. A pointbased offset optimization is also developed to refine the reconstructed complete body shape, enabling accurate regression of measurement values. To train the proposed model,
a new large-scale dataset, consisting of 300K samples, was
synthesized. The proposed model was validated using two
publicly available real-world datasets and was compared
with different relevant methods. Extensive experimental results demonstrate that MeasureXpert achieves superior performance compared to the reference methods. The code and
dataset are available at: MeasureXpertProject.
