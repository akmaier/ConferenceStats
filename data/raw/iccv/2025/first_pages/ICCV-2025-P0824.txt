Abstract
Existing hypergraph clustering methods typically assume
that node attributes are fully available. However, in realworld scenarios, missing node attributes are common for
the sake of privacy or due to data noise. While some
approaches attempt to handle missing attributes in traditional graphs, they are not designed for hypergraphs,
which encode higher-order relationships and introduce
additional challenges. To bridge this gap, we propose
Hypergraph Clustering Network with Partial Attribute
Imputation (HCN-PAI). Specifically, we first leverage
higher-order neighborhood propagation to impute missing node attributes by minimizing the Dirichlet energy, ensuring smooth feature propagation across the hypergraph.
Next, we introduce a hypergraph smoothing preprocessing
that efficiently captures structural information, replacing
the hypergraph convolution operation, and significantly reducing computational costs. Finally, we design a duallevel contrastive mechanism, which employs two independent MLPs to encode node representations into two distinct views and enforces consistency at both node and hyperedge levels. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets validate the effectiveness and superiority of
our proposed method.

Building on the success of GNNs, researchers have
developed Hypergraph Neural Networks (HGNNs) to extend message passing mechanisms to hypergraphs, enabling
more expressive representation learning. HGNNs employ
a two-stage message passing mechanism: first, hyperedge
representations are iteratively updated by aggregating information from connected nodes, and then node representations are refined by incorporating information from their incident hyperedges. Despite their strong performance, these
methods rely heavily on labeled data for training and optimization. However, acquiring high-quality labeled data
can be costly and time-consuming, particularly in domains
requiring expert annotation. This challenge has driven increasing interest in unsupervised approaches, such as hypergraph clustering, which seeks to reveal meaningful structures within hypergraphs without relying on labeled supervision.

in hypergraph data. Existing hypergraph clustering methods can be broadly categorized into traditional approaches
[39, 41, 42] and deep learning-based approaches [7, 14, 17].

1. Introduction
Graphs serve as a fundamental data structure, and Graph
Neural Networks (GNNs) [12, 30, 34] have demonstrated
strong performance in modeling graph structures and node
attributes. However, traditional graphs are limited to pairwise relationships, making it challenging to capture complex multi-node interactions. To address this limitation,
hypergraphs, where each hyperedge can connect multiple
nodes simultaneously, have gained increasing attention in
fields such as social networks [16, 37, 43], recommendation
systems [10, 13, 36], and biological systems [4, 5, 19].
