ISP typically consists of a series of image processing
modules [5] and is often treated as a black box, with only
those ISP hyperparameters closely associated with the functional modules [3] available for adjustment. These hyperparameters influence both the visual quality of the output images and the performance of subsequent downstream visual
tasks [44]. Traditionally, these hyperparameters are manually tuned by imaging experts based on their experience
and visual perception [4]. However, this process is both
time-consuming and labor-intensive, and the hyperparameters adjusted by experts tend to be biased toward human
visual preferences, which may not be optimal for high-level
visual task scenarios [37, 44].
To address these challenges, several approaches have
been proposed for ISP hyperparameter optimization. Some
methods [19, 29] rely on search strategies like evolutionary algorithms and multi-objective optimization, which are
time-consuming and computationally intensive. Recent
learning-based methods [22, 23, 32, 33, 37] train neural
network models to optimize hyperparameters, as shown in

which fails to fully leverage multimodal information. Image
data alone provide only basic visual cues (e.g., brightness,
noise, and color), limiting the ability to capture the richer
image content and quality characteristics, thus constraining
the accuracy and robustness of the optimization process.

The image signal processing (ISP) pipeline is responsible
for converting the RAW images collected from the sensor
into high-quality RGB images. It contains a series of image processing modules and associated ISP hyperparameters. Recent learning-based approaches aim to automate
ISP hyperparameter optimization using solely image data.
However, their unimodal nature limits their ability to capture richer contextual information, reducing robustness and
adaptability across diverse application scenarios. To address this limitation, we propose a Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM)-guided ISP hyperparameter optimization framework, which integrates textual insights generated by MLLMs into the optimization process. By incorporating both high-level semantic cues and low-level image
quality descriptors, our method enhances contextual understanding and task adaptability. Additionally, we introduce a
Dynamic Pair Generation (DPG) refinement strategy based
on Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), facilitating efficient preference alignment without the need for extensive
human-labeled data. This two-stage framework not only improves the directional consistency of optimization but also
significantly reduces the computational and data preparation overhead. We validate our proposed methods on both
high-level and low-level vision tasks, demonstrating superior performance compared to existing methods.
