Probabilistic Inertial Poser (ProbIP): Uncertainty-aware Human Motion
Modeling from Sparse Inertial Sensors
Min Kim, Younho Jeon, Sungho Jo∗
KAIST, Daejeon, Republic of Korea
{swh02004, 117jyh}@gmail.com, shjo@kaist.ac.kr
*corresponding authors

Abstract
Wearable Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) allow nonintrusive motion tracking, but limited sensor placements
can introduce uncertainty in capturing detailed full-body
movements. Existing methods mitigate this issue by selecting more physically plausible motion patterns but do not directly address inherent uncertainties in the data. We introduce the Probabilistic Inertial Poser (ProbIP), a novel probabilistic model that transforms sparse IMU data into human
motion predictions without physical constraints. ProbIP
utilizes RU-Mamba blocks to predict a matrix Fisher distribution over rotations, effectively estimating both rotation
matrices and associated uncertainties. To refine motion distribution through layers, our Progressive Distribution Narrowing (PDN) technique enables stable learning across a
diverse range of motions. Experimental results demonstrate
that ProbIP achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple public datasets with six and fewer IMU sensors. Our
contributions include the development of ProbIP with RUMamba blocks for probabilistic motion estimation, applying
Progressive Distribution Narrowing (PDN) for uncertainty
reduction, and evidence of superior results with six and reduced sensor configurations. The code will be available at
https://github.com/MinKim14/ProbIP-ICCV2025.

Minimizing the number of sensors attached to the body
is essential for a comfortable and practical wearable-based
human motion estimation. A key challenge in this area
arises from the uncertainty of unseen joint rotations due to
limited sensor placement [9, 10, 19]. Since IMU sensors
measure acceleration (the second derivative of joint positions) with noise, current motion predictions rely heavily
on the accuracy of preceding frames in real-time motion estimation. To address this, researchers have applied various
constraints to select the most plausible poses among multiple candidates. For instance, previous methods have introduced contact probability for feet [31] to counteract IMU
signal drift, ensuring that generated motions remain stable
when contact probability is high. This concept has been extended to full-body contact probability for post-processing
motions involving surface contact, such as sitting or lying
down [11]. Furthermore, physical properties like ground

and non-intrusively capturing these motions in daily life is
crucial for advancing our understanding of human behavior,
with applications from healthcare monitoring to real-world
human-computer interactions.
