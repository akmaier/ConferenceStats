Abstract
Specular highlights, though valuable for human perception,
are often undesirable in computer vision and graphics tasks
as they can obscure surface details and affect analysis. Existing methods rely on multi-stage pipelines or multi-label
datasets, making training difficult. In this study, we propose
a one-step diffusion-based model for specular highlight removal, leveraging a pre-trained diffusion-based image generation model with an adaptation mechanism to enhance efficiency and adaptability. To further improve the adaptation
process, we introduce ProbLoRA, a novel modification of
Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), designed to adapt the diffusion model for highlight removal effectively. Our approach
surpasses existing methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both quantitative metrics and visual quality.
Extensive experiments on synthetic and real-world datasets
demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, highlighting
its robustness and generalization capabilities.

1. Introduction
Specular highlights are a natural optical phenomenon in human vision. However, they can be undesired artifacts in
computer vision that obscure critical details, particularly
when analyzing reflective or glossy surfaces such as documents, human faces, and other specular materials. Moreover, their removal is essential for various image editing
tasks, including intrinsic image decomposition [37], recoloring [1], light source estimation [11], and material recognition [21]. Consequently, specular highlight removal has
remained a challenging and active research topic for years.
Traditional methods [10, 14, 28, 34] rely on low-level
intensity and chromaticity information, limiting their ability to leverage high-level semantic cues. As a result, they
often fail to remove specular highlights effectively. Deep
learning-based methods [2, 27, 32, 35] have achieved significant advancements in specular highlight removal. However, their generalization is often constrained by the limited diversity of training datasets. To address this issue,

In this study, we propose a one-step specular highlight
removal model that leverages Stable Diffusion Turbo (SD
Turbo) [25], a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion-based
model. Instead of training a model from scratch, we introduce new adapter layers that condition the input image,
drawing inspiration from recent image-to-image translation
models [23]. Specifically, we modify the Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [9] technique and introduce Probabilistic
Row-Wise Adaptation LoRA (ProbLoRA), a novel adapter
mechanism that enhances adaptability while maintaining efficiency. These adapter layers guide the diffusion process
to generate a specular highlight-free output, enabling efficient and high-quality highlight removal while harnessing
