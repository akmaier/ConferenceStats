Abstract
Despite the success of adversarial training on small
datasets, applying it to large-scale datasets like ImageNet
remains challenging. Previous attempts using synthetic
data show limited improvements. This work investigates
the impact of synthetic data scaling, model scaling, and
training strategies on adversarial training with ImageNet,
providing deeper insights into large-scale robustness. During the process, we observe a notable phenomenon of loss
oscillation, leading to adversarial overfitting, and propose
strategies to mitigate it. Experimental results show that,
under AutoAttack on ImageNet-1K, our method achieves a
robust accuracy of 71.54%. Our findings highlight the crucial role of synthetic data and model scaling in enhancing
adversarial robustness on large-scale benchmarks and provide a new direction for training robust visual representations at scale.

1. Introduction
Adversarial training, which incorporates adversarial examples into the training process, is a cornerstone of defending deep neural networks against malicious attacks [11, 16].
The robustness it confers is strongly linked to data availability [21, 24], leading to various strategies that leverage additional data [18, 26, 35]. Among these, using synthetic data generated by diffusion models has emerged as
a particularly promising, learning-based data augmentation
technique [27]. This approach has become a standard and
effective practice for improving adversarial robustness on
smaller datasets like CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, without
needing external datasets [4, 12, 19, 27].
In contrast, this success has not translated to large-scale
datasets like ImageNet. Prior work that generated 5M syn* Equal contribution.

Our main contributions are as follows:
1. Significant Robustness Gains via Scaled Synthetic
Data. Our synthetic data scaling methodology significantly advances the performance for methods without external data. Our largest model reaches 71.54% l∞ -robust
accuracy on ImageNet under AutoAttack, outperforming
prior benchmarks on RobustBench [7].
2. Analysis and Mitigation of Large-Model Training Instability. We identify and analyze loss oscillation, a critical training instability that emerges in large-scale adversarial training. To address this, we introduce the Equality
Rate (EQ Rate) as a diagnostic tool and propose StablePGD, an adaptive sign scaling method that effectively
mitigates the instability, enabling robust convergence for
large models.
3. Efficient Performance without External Data. Our
approach achieves strong robust performance, outperforming methods like AdvXL [28] that rely on massive
external datasets (1B samples), while using only 50M
internally-generated samples and significantly less computation. This demonstrates an efficient pathway to high
robustness, highlighting the advantages of a targeted data
strategy.
