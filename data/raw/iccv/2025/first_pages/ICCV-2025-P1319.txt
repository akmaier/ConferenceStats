Abstract
Driven by large-scale model iterations, the inference speed
and generalization ability of 3D model generation have improved significantly. However, the quality of existing methods still falls short of enabling direct use without postprocessing. Common issues include insufficient texture
clarity, loss of semantic information, lack of fine-grained
detail, and the generation of redundant artifacts. Moreover,
current approaches focus solely on producing static structures, where individual components remain non-movable,
without considering functional applications in the generation process. To address these limitations, we draw inspiration from LEGO-like modular construction and decompose
complex models into semantically functional components.
We propose LEGO-Maker, a novel framework that reformulates the text-to-3D task into a three-stage process: target
image generation, functional semantic decomposition, and
multi-task 3D generation with structured fusion. Leveraging a reorganized high-quality 3D dataset, we train a Diffusion model and a semantic segmentation model tailored
for 3D generation tasks. Additionally, we design a motiondriven mechanism to introduce action sequences for functionally interactive modules after model fusion. Experimental results demonstrate that, compared to existing methods,
our approach significantly enhances semantic understanding, model detail quality, and text consistency while showcasing direct applicability across various scenarios.

1. Introduction

Fusion [30] introduced Score Distillation Sampling (SDS),
leveraging pretrained 2D diffusion models for text-to-3D
generation without supervision. Subsequent works, including ProlificDreamer [41] and Magic3D [21], enhanced quality and efficiency through variational score distillation and
two-stage optimization, though inference remained slow.
TRELLIS [42] introduced Structured LATent (SLAT), integrating multi-view features with rectified flow transformers
for direct 3D generation in minutes. SPAR3D [17] further
refined this process with a two-stage pipeline, using diffusion models to generate sparse point clouds and refine them
into detailed meshes, achieving inference in seconds.

challenges, but as shown in Figure 1, their outputs still require extensive post-processing. Common issues include
blurry textures [30], missing semantic details [25] (e.g., luggage racks, roof lights and double-door structures), lack of
