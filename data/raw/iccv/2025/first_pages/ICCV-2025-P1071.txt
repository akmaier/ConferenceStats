Abstract
Pathological image has been recognized as the gold standard for cancer diagnosis for more than a century. However, some internal regions of pathological images may inevitably exhibit various degradation issues, including low
resolution, image blurring, and image noising, which will
affect disease diagnosis, staging, and risk stratification. Existing pathological image restoration methods were mainly
based on generative adversarial networks (GANs) to improve image quality, which are limited by the inherent instability and loss of structural details, often resulting in artifacts in the restored images. Large scale of whole slide
images (WSIs) also makes it hard for efficient processing
and restoration. To address these limitations, we propose
a conditional visual autoregressive model (CVARPath) for
next-scale token prediction, guided by the degraded tokens from the current scale. We introduce a novel framework that employs quantified encoders specifically designed
for pathological image generation, which learns consistent sparse vocabulary tokens through self-supervised contrastive learning. Furthermore, our method efficiently compresses image patches into compact degraded sparse tokens at smaller scales and reconstructs high-quality largescale WSIs. This is achieved using only an 8×8 vocabulary index for 256×256 images while maintaining minimal
reconstruction loss. Experimental results demonstrate that
our approach significantly enhances image quality, achieving an approximately 30% improvement in mean Fréchet
inception distance (FID) compared to popular conditional

However, pathological images are often affected by various image degradation issues that can compromise their

result in inappropriate therapeutic decisions, and ultimately
affect patient outcomes. Therefore, it is necessary to address these challenges to ensure reliable and accurate pathological diagnoses.

1. Introduction

Traditional approaches to pathological image restoration
have relied on classical image processing techniques, such
as Wiener filtering [5], total variation minimization [49],
and histogram equalization [8]. While these methods can
mitigate certain types of degradation, they often fail to handle the complex and diverse nature of pathological images.
For example, they may struggle to preserve fine-grained
details or adapt to varying degradation patterns. More recently, deep learning-based methods, such as generative adversarial networks (GANs) [16] and latent diffusion models
(LDMs) [33], have shown promise in addressing these limitations. However, these approaches still face challenges,
including limited generalization to unseen data, high computational costs, high inference time costs and difficulty in
