Abstract
We explore a quaternion adjugate matrix-based representation for rotational motion in the Perspective-n-Point (PnP)
problem. Leveraging quadratic quaternion terms within a
Determinant Ratio Matrix (DRaM) estimation framework,
we extend its application to perspective scenarios, providing a robust and efficient initialization for iterative PnP
pose estimation. Notably, by solving the orthographic projection least-squares problem, DRaM provides a reliable
initialization that enhances the accuracy and stability of iterative PnP solvers. Experiments on synthetic and real data
demonstrate its efficiency, accuracy, and robustness, particularly under high noise conditions. Furthermore, our nonminimal formulation ensures numerical stability, making it
effective for real-world applications.

1. Introduction
The estimation of a calibrated camera’s pose, which determines its spatial orientation and position, is fundamental in fields such as geometric perception, simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), and visual relocalization.
This task, known as the Perspective-n-Point (PnP) problem, seeks to compute the camera’s SE(3) pose from a
set of 2D-3D correspondences under the pinhole camera
model. A significant challenge in solving the PnP problem is the encoding and manipulation of the camera’s rotational component, which requires maintaining the constraints of the SO(3) manifold and addressing potential singularities or symmetries associated with the chosen parametric representation. In this work, we introduce the application of the quaternion adjugate (or adjoint) matrix
within the context of PnP solvers. We demonstrate how
this over-determined parametrization effectively models orthographic projections, providing a robust and near-optimal

initialization for pose estimation.

high-order polynomial equations or linearizing the problem,
which can be computationally expensive and susceptible to
