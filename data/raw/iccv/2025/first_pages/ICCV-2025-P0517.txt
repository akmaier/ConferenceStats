leveraging monocular depth estimation to fuse multi-view information into a global coordinate system represented by point clouds. These
fused point clouds serve as inputs for pedestrian localization. Stereo-based methods [22] struggle with sparse views and severe occlusions,
causing inaccurate geometry predictions. Monocular depth estimation methods [3, 15, 47] often lack multi-view consistency, leading to
misaligned point clouds—e.g., two people captured by three cameras are projected as separate targets. Baseline UMPD [25] struggles
with sparse view reconstruction; erroneous projections increase false positives. In contrast, our approach ensures spatially consistent depth
predictions across views, enabling more accurate detection, as indicated in better MODA scores [36].

Multiview pedestrian detection typically involves two
stages: human modeling and pedestrian localization. Human modeling represents pedestrians in 3D space by fusing
multiview information, making its quality crucial for detection accuracy. However, existing methods often introduce
noise and have low precision. While some approaches reduce noise by fitting on costly multiview 3D annotations,
they often struggle to generalize across diverse scenes. To
