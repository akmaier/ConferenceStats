Abstract
Current virtual try-on methods primarily enhance performance through network optimization, employing strategies
such as coarse-to-fine structures and ReferenceNet to inject clothing information. However, these methods are often
constrained by the limited quantity and diversity of training
samples, which ultimately restricts their potential for further improvement. To address this challenge, we propose a
unified, mask-free virtual try-on framework. Our approach
leverages the inherent strengths of latent diffusion models
to enhance the capability of each pipeline component to
accurately model the target distribution, thereby achieving
superior performance. Specifically, we introduce a textdriven pseudo-input preparation strategy that significantly
increases the diversity of clothing regions within the generated person pseudo-samples. This encourages the generator to focus on variations in these areas and improves
the model’s generalization capability. Within the generator
itself, we develop a gated manipulation mechanism to prevent weight forgetting and reduce training costs. Furthermore, we incorporate a texture-aware injection module to
explicitly integrate human-perceptible clothing texture information into the generation process. During inference, we
propose a refining conditional inference strategy that mitigates the randomness introduced by Gaussian noise. This
effectively preserves identity information and fine clothing
details in the final results. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art
virtual try-on approaches.

Previous VTON methods are broadly categorized into
mask-based and mask-free paradigms. Mask-based approaches [3, 14, 22, 37] typically operate in a selfsupervised fashion: a clothing-agnostic mask (Figure 2)
is first applied to remove the garment region and adjacent
body parts from the reference image, and the generator is
then trained to inpaint the masked areas, thereby learning
to perform the virtual try-on task. Although effective and
widely adopted, these methods rely heavily on obtaining
accurate masks for each reference person; even minor mask
errors can introduce conspicuous artifacts [13, 21]. To mitigate mask-induced artifacts, mask-free methods grounded
in pseudo-sample [8, 13, 18] have been proposed. A maskbased VTON model is first pre-trained to synthesize tryon images for arbitrary garments on the reference person,
yielding paired pseudo-samples. These pseudo-samples are
then fed as inputs, while the original reference image serves
as the reconstruction target (ground truth), to train a main
generator. Although this pipeline obviates the need for precise masks, they are limited to a single, fixed transformation
for each pseudo-sample/reference-person pair. Moreover,
because both garments and reference images are typically
drawn from the same dataset, the generator overfits to these
specific items and generalizes poorly to unseen clothing.
There are also some other methods attempt to boost
performance through architectural improvements—such as
coarse-to-fine networks and ReferenceNet-based clothingfeature injection [22, 37, 40]. Yet these refinements remain
constrained by the input materials (human parsing, etc),
causing the generator to converge to sub-optimal solutions.
Finally, the randomness of Gaussian-noise sampling during
inference further degrades output quality [36]—a problem
often overlooked by prior work.
