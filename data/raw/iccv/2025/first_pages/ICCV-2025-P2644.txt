Video Understanding

Abstract
High-quality image-text data is critical for VisionLanguage Models (VLMs), yet traditional image-based pretraining is resource-intensive and fails to capture the temporal dynamics needed for video understanding. To address
this, we introduce video pretraining to enhance VLMs with
temporal reasoning. We propose Causal Hierarchical Aggregation, a novel method that efficiently processes video
by separating computationally heavy spatial encoding from
lightweight temporal propagation. This technique builds hierarchical receptive fields, enabling effective learning from
large-scale video data. Scaling our method to over 100 billion video tokens, we achieve state-of-the-art performance
and high throughput on both image and video understanding tasks (Figure 1). Our approach offers a scalable solution to advance multimodal learning for dynamic contexts. Our code and pretrained models will be released
at https://github.com/invictus717/LLaVAPrime.

The advancement of vision-language models (VLMs) significantly enhanced the capabilities of the large language
models (LLM) in multimodal understanding [2, 7, 26, 44,
46, 56, 61, 66]. Existing VLMs, including LLaVA [44, 46],
Gemini [61], and Qwen-VL [2], primarily rely on crossmodal alignment between vision encoders and language
models. As image-text datasets used for pretraining scaleup, VLMs demonstrate impressive scalability in universal
vision-language understanding, with significant advancements in visual question answering [66], visual knowledge
learning [44], and visual agents [2, 65].
The effectiveness of this development approach is intrinsically tied to the quality of the pretraining data, specifically the image-text pairs, which must feature both high
visual fidelity and accurate, contextually relevant captions.

However, current pretraining datasets face two critical limitations. First, creating high-quality image-text pairs with
coherent natural language captions is an extensive endeavor,
demanding significant financial, computational, and storage resources. Each aspect of data quality refinement adds
both cost and complexity, making high-quality datasets a
challenging and often impractical standard for widespread
use. Second, and more fundamentally, we observe that
while these high-quality datasets enhance static image understanding, they fall short in supporting dynamic video
comprehension within vision-language models. Image-text
pairs, by nature, capture isolated moments without temporal
context, which limits the models’ ability to perceive sequential changes essential for video understanding.
