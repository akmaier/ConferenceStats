1. Introduction

Significant efforts [38, 46] have been made to develop
effective adversarial camouflage, typically generated via
backpropagation during attack optimization. Existing methods focus on differentiable rendering pipelines [34, 35]
or texture projection alignment [46] to enhance robustness and transferability from virtual to real-world environments. However, updating textures with gradients from
multiple viewpoints introduces conflicts, and failing to resolve them leads to suboptimal performance. In real-world
settings, surface illumination varies with light sources and
intensities. Simple rendering methods, which directly map
RGB values to the final image, fail to account for these
variations, causing discrepancies between optimization and
evaluation. Existing approaches often rely on neural networks [46] to model lighting interactions, but at high computational costs with limited generalization. Moreover, we
identify inconsistencies in sampling density during rendering, which cause non-uniform texture updates. This disrupt
smoothness and degrading attack performance, particularly
at greater distances where details are lost.

Object detection [11, 13, 21, 31] is essential for applications
like autonomous driving [8] and scene understanding [7],
where security and reliability are critical [17–20, 25]. A major threat is adversarial attacks [29, 42, 45], where attackers
manipulate real-world objects or surroundings to mislead
detection models, causing misclassification or missed detections. Such attacks pose serious safety risks, potentially
leading to traffic accidents.
