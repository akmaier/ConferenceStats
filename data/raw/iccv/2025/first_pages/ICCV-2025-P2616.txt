Abstract
Single-view 3D reconstruction aims to recover the complete 3D geometry and appearance of objects from a single RGB image. Due to incomplete image information and
ambiguity, this task remains challenging. Existing methods
struggle with the trade-off between local detail and global
topology, and with interference from early RGB–depth fusion in signed distance function optimization. To address
these challenges, we propose Dual-S3D, a novel framework for single-view 3D reconstruction. Our method employs a hierarchical dual-path feature extraction strategy
based on stages that utilize convolutional neural networks
to anchor local geometric details. In contrast, subsequent stages leverage a Transformer integrated with selective state-space model to capture global topology, enhancing scene understanding and feature representation. Additionally, we design an auxiliary branch that progressively
fuses precomputed depth features with pixel-level features
to decouple visual and geometric cues effectively. Extensive experiments on the 3D-FRONT and Pix3D datasets
demonstrate that our approach significantly outperforms
existing methods—reducing chamfer distance by 51%, increasing F-score by 33.6%, and improving normal consistency by 10.3%—thus achieving state-of-the-art reconstruction quality. Code is available here.

1. Introduction
The single-view 3D reconstruction aims to recover the
three-dimensional geometry and appearance of a scene from
a single RGB image and its corresponding camera parameters, providing critical support for applications in virtual reality [15], autonomous driving [4], robotics [24], and more.

Since a single image captures only partial information, the
model must reconstruct the visible regions and infer the
structure of occluded areas, posing significant uncertainty
and a challenge for high-fidelity reconstruction.
Current mainstream approaches primarily rely on convolutional neural networks (CNNs), transformers, or statespace models (SSMs) for feature extraction, yet they face
two significant issues. First, balancing local detail with
global topology is challenging: conventional CNNs can
capture fine local geometric features (e.g., edges and corners) [10], but their inherent local bias limits the construction of overall topology. In contrast, transformers or SSMs
excel at modeling long-range dependencies [30] but often
overlook fine details [25]. Naively stacking these modules can lead to feature conflicts and substantially increase
computational costs. Second, many methods use the same
RGB features for decoding both geometry and texture [40].
This approach allows rich texture signals in highly textured
regions to interfere with geometric recovery, destabilizing
signed distance function (SDF) optimization [36] and reducing reconstruction accuracy.

fusion. Our framework takes the input image and camera
pose and directly predicts the SDF values and color for each
