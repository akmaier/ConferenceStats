Abstract
In many fine-grained object recognition datasets, image
orientation (left/right) might vary from sample to sample.
Since handcrafted descriptors such as SIFT are not reversal invariant, the stability of image representation based
on them is consequently limited. A popular solution is to
augment the datasets by adding a left-right reversed copy
for each original image. This strategy improves recognition
accuracy to some extent, but also brings the price of almost
doubled time and memory consumptions.
In this paper, we present RIDE (Reversal Invariant Descriptor Enhancement) for fine-grained object recognition.
RIDE is a generalized algorithm which cancels out the
impact of image reversal by estimating the orientation of
local descriptors, and guarantees to produce the identical
representation for an image and its left-right reversed copy.
Experimental results reveal the consistent accuracy gain of
RIDE with various types of descriptors. We also provide
insightful discussions on the working mechanism of RIDE
and its generalization to other applications.

algorithm in which local features are extracted, encoded and
summarized into global image representation.
