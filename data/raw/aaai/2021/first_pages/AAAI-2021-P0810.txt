Online optimization is modeled as a repeated game between
a learner and an adversary (Hazan 2016). At the t-th round,
t ∈ [T ] , {1, . . . , T }, the learner selects an action xt from a
convex set X ⊆ Rn based on the information from previous
rounds. Then, the adversary reveals a convex function ft :
X → R to the learner that incurs the loss ft (xt ). The goal
of online learning is to minimize the regret, which is the
difference between the cumulative loss of the learner and
that of a comparator sequence in hindsight. Depending on
the comparator sequence, the regret can be either static or
dynamic. The static regret is defined with respect to a fixed
comparator as follows
