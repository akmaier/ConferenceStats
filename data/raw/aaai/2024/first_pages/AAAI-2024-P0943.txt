Introduction
The goal of unsupervised graph anomaly detection (GAD)
is to identify rare patterns that deviate from the majority patterns in a graph, which has been extensively applied in diverse domains, such as fraud detection (Abdallah, Maarof, and Zainal 2016; Cheng et al. 2020; Dou et al.
2020) and social network (Fan, Zhang, and Li 2020; Duan
et al. 2023). Recently, reconstruction-based Graph Neural
Networks (GNNs) methods have achieved great success and
have become the mainstream approach. The common assumption is that normal nodes are easier to be reconstructed
than abnormal nodes. On this basis, such methods usually
train a graph autoencoder and determine anomalies according to the magnitude of the reconstruction errors.
