of models involves aligning LLMs with accurate knowledge sources (Yang et al. 2023; Wen et al. 2024; Xu et al.

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in complex reasoning tasks. However, they
can be easily misled by unfaithful arguments during conversations, even when their original statements are correct. To
this end, we investigate the problem of maintaining faithful integrity in LLMs. This involves ensuring that LLMs adhere to their faithful statements in the face of opposing arguments and are able to correct their incorrect statements
when presented with faithful arguments. In this work, we
propose a novel framework, named Alignment for Faithful
Integrity with Confidence Estimation (AFICE), which aims
to align the LLM responses with faithful integrity. Specifically, AFICE first designs a Bilateral Confidence Estimation
(BCE) approach for estimating the uncertainty of each response generated by the LLM given a specific context, which
simultaneously estimate the model’s confidence to the question based on the internal states during decoding as well as to
the answer based on cumulative probability ratios. With the
BCE, we construct a conversational preference dataset composed of context, original statement, and argument, which is
adopted for aligning the LLM for faithful integrity using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Extensive experimental
results on a wide range of benchmarks demonstrate significant improvements in the LLM’s ability to maintain faithful
responses when encountering opposing arguments, ensuring
both the practical utility and trustworthiness of LLMs in complex interactive settings.
